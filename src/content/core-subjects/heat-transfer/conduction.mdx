## What is Conduction?

Conduction is the mode of heat transfer that occurs through direct molecular interaction within a material without bulk movement of the material itself.

Heat always flows: from higher temperature to lower temperature .

until thermal equilibrium is reached.

---

## Everyday Examples

- Heating of a metal spoon inside hot tea
- Heat transfer through reactor walls
- Heat loss from pipelines
- Heat transfer through insulation

<IndustrialInsight>
In industrial systems, conduction directly affects heat exchanger efficiency, insulation performance, furnace operation, and reactor thermal stability.
</IndustrialInsight>

---

## Mechanism of Conduction

In solids, conduction occurs due to:

- molecular vibration
- electron movement
- energy transfer between adjacent particles

Metals generally have higher thermal conductivity because free electrons transfer energy rapidly.

---

## Fourier's Law of Heat Conduction

The rate of heat transfer by conduction is governed by Fourier’s Law.

### One-Dimensional Steady State Equation

<EquationBlock
  title="Fourier's Law Equation"
  equation="Q = -kA (dT/dx)"
  description="Heat transfer rate is proportional to temperature gradient and thermal conductivity."
/>

Where:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Q | Heat transfer rate |
| k | Thermal conductivity |
| A | Area normal to heat flow |
| dT/dx | Temperature gradient |

<WarningBox>
High temperature gradients create thermal stress.
</WarningBox>

<IndustrialInsight>
A high thermal conductivity material transfers heat rapidly. Copper and aluminum are widely used in heat exchangers because of their high thermal conductivity.
</IndustrialInsight>

## Thermal Conductivity

Thermal conductivity represents the ability of a material to conduct heat.

### High Thermal Conductivity Materials

- Copper
- Aluminum
- Silver

### Low Thermal Conductivity Materials

- Wood
- Rubber
- Fiberglass
- Ceramic insulation

---

## Factors Affecting Conduction

### 1. Temperature Gradient

Higher temperature difference increases heat transfer rate.

### 2. Material Thermal Conductivity

Higher conductivity increases conduction.

### 3. Thickness of Material

Larger thickness reduces heat transfer.

### 4. Area of Heat Transfer

Larger area increases conduction rate.

---

## Types of Conduction

### Steady-State Conduction

Temperature distribution does not change with time.

### Transient Conduction

Temperature changes with time.

Examples:
- Heating of metal blocks
- Cooling of reactor walls
- Startup heating processes

## Industrial Applications

### Heat Exchangers

Conduction occurs through tube walls between hot and cold fluids.

### Furnaces

Heat transfers through refractory linings.

### Pipelines

Heat loss occurs through pipe walls.

### Reactor Jackets

Thermal energy transfers between jacket fluid and reactor contents.

## Common Industrial Problems

### Fouling

Deposits increase thermal resistance and reduce heat transfer efficiency.

### Insulation Damage

Causes heat loss and energy wastage.

### Thermal Stress

Uneven temperature distribution can crack equipment walls.

<IndustrialInsight>
Thermal stress management is critical in high-temperature reactors and heat exchangers to avoid equipment failure and shutdowns.
</IndustrialInsight>
