# Stefan-Boltzmann Law

<TopicRoadmap />


## Why does radiation become important in furnaces?

At room temperature, radiation heat transfer is usually small.

However, inside:

- Furnaces
- Fired heaters
- Boilers
- Combustion chambers

radiation becomes the dominant mode of heat transfer.

Why?

The answer lies in the **Stefan-Boltzmann Law**.

Unlike conduction and convection, radiation depends on the **fourth power of temperature**.

This causes radiation to increase extremely rapidly as temperature rises.

---

# Stefan-Boltzmann Law for a Black Body

The total radiation emitted by a black body is given by:

here emissivity is always 1 hence not included .


$$
q = \sigma A T^4
$$


Where:

| Symbol | Description |
|----------|----------|
| \(q\) | Radiation heat transfer rate (W) |
| \(\sigma\) | Stefan-Boltzmann Constant |
| \(A\) | Surface Area (m²) |
| \(T\) | Absolute Temperature (K) |

---

# Stefan-Boltzmann Law for Real Surfaces

Real surfaces do not emit as much radiation as a black body.

Therefore emissivity is included:


$$
q = \varepsilon \sigma A T^4
$$


Where:

| Symbol | Description |
|----------|----------|
| \(\varepsilon\) | Emissivity |
| \(\sigma\) | Stefan-Boltzmann Constant |
| \(A\) | Surface Area |
| \(T\) | Absolute Temperature |

---

# The Most Important Part: \(T^4\)

Notice the term:

$$
T^4
$$

Temperature is raised to the fourth power.

This makes radiation extremely sensitive to temperature.

---

# Understanding the T⁴ Effect

Suppose the temperature doubles.

Then:

$$
q \propto T^4
$$

Therefore:

$$
q_{new}
=
(2T)^4
=
16T^4
$$

Thus:

> Doubling temperature increases radiation by 16 times.

---

# Radiation Growth with Temperature

| Temperature (K) | Relative Radiation |
|----------|----------|
| 300 | 1 |
| 600 | 16 |
| 900 | 81 |
| 1200 | 256 |

Even a moderate increase in temperature causes a dramatic increase in radiation heat transfer.

---

# Why Furnaces Depend on Radiation

Consider a refinery furnace.

Many students assume:

> The flame heats the tubes mainly through hot gases.

Only partially true.

In reality:

- Flames radiate.
- Refractory walls radiate.
- Hot combustion gases radiate.

The process tubes absorb this radiation.

At furnace temperatures, radiation often contributes the majority of the heat transfer.

---

# Industrial Insight: Glowing Hot Objects

As temperature increases:

- Radiation intensity increases rapidly.
- Visible emission begins to appear.

Examples:

| Object | Appearance |
|----------|----------|
| Steam Pipe | No Glow |
| Furnace Tube | Red Glow |
| Molten Steel | Orange Glow |
| Welding Arc | Bright White |

This occurs because both radiation intensity and wavelength distribution change with temperature.

---

# Why Insulation Saves Energy

Consider an uninsulated steam pipe.

```text
250°C Pipe
     ↓
Atmosphere
```

Large radiation losses occur.

Now add insulation.

```text
250°C Steam
      ↓
    Pipe
      ↓
 Insulation
      ↓
50°C Surface
      ↓
 Atmosphere
```

The outer surface temperature decreases significantly.

Since radiation depends on:

$$
T^4
$$

the heat loss drops dramatically.

This is one reason insulation saves fuel and operating costs.

# Engineering Applications

Stefan-Boltzmann Law is used in:

- Furnace design
- Boiler analysis
- Fired heaters
- Flare radiation studies
- Spacecraft thermal control
- Radiation shields
- Insulation calculations

# Engineering Perspective

Whenever a process engineer sees:

- A furnace
- A flare stack
- A glowing reactor wall
- A fired heater

they should immediately think:

> Radiation is likely dominating because of the T⁴ effect.

Ignoring radiation at high temperatures can lead to serious design errors.

# Key Takeaways

- Stefan-Boltzmann Law predicts total radiation emitted by a surface.
- For a black body:

$$
q = \sigma A T^4
$$

- For a real surface:

$$
q = \varepsilon \sigma A T^4
$$

- Radiation is proportional to the fourth power of temperature.
- Doubling temperature increases radiation by 16 times.
- Radiation becomes dominant in furnaces and other high-temperature equipment.
- The T⁴ effect is the primary reason radiation is so important in industrial heat transfer.

---

# Summary

Stefan-Boltzmann Law relates the total radiation emitted by a surface to its temperature and area. For real surfaces, emissivity accounts for deviations from ideal black-body behavior. The most important feature of the law is the T⁴ dependence, which causes radiation heat transfer to increase dramatically with temperature. This explains why radiation becomes the dominant heat transfer mechanism in furnaces, fired heaters, boilers, and other high-temperature industrial systems.
