# Thermal Radiation


<TopicRoadmap />

## Why can you feel heat from a furnace without touching it?

Suppose you stand several meters away from a hot furnace.

Even though you are not touching the furnace, and there may be little air movement between you and the furnace, you can still feel intense heat.

How is energy reaching your body?

The answer is **thermal radiation**.

Unlike conduction and convection, thermal radiation does not require physical contact or fluid motion. Energy is transferred directly through electromagnetic waves emitted by hot surfaces.

---

# What Is Thermal Radiation?

Thermal radiation is the emission of electromagnetic energy by a body due to its temperature.

Whenever a body possesses thermal energy, its atoms, molecules, and electrons are continuously moving and vibrating.

These accelerating charged particles emit electromagnetic waves, which carry energy away from the surface.

This emitted energy is known as **thermal radiation**.

---

# Origin of Thermal Radiation

Inside every material:

- Atoms vibrate continuously.
- Molecules rotate and vibrate.
- Electrons move around atomic nuclei.

As temperature increases:

- Molecular motion increases.
- Internal energy increases.
- Radiation emission increases.

Therefore, thermal radiation is a direct consequence of the thermal motion of particles inside matter.

---

# Every Object Emits Radiation

One of the most important concepts in heat transfer is:

> Every object above absolute zero emits thermal radiation.

Absolute zero is:

$$
0 K
$$

At any temperature above 0 K, atoms and molecules possess energy and therefore emit radiation.

This means radiation is not limited to very hot objects.

Even objects at room temperature continuously emit thermal radiation.

---

# Common Examples

## Human Body

The human body operates at approximately:

$$
310 K
$$

Even though we cannot see it, our bodies continuously emit infrared radiation.

This is how thermal imaging cameras detect people.

---

## Steam Pipe

A hot steam pipe continuously emits thermal radiation to its surroundings.

Some of the heat lost from uninsulated pipes occurs through radiation.

---

## Reactor Vessel

A reactor operating at elevated temperatures radiates energy to nearby equipment and the atmosphere.

This radiation contributes to overall heat loss.

---

## Furnace

A furnace emits enormous amounts of thermal radiation.

At high temperatures, radiation often becomes the dominant mode of heat transfer.

---

# Thermal Radiation in Daily Life

Examples include:

- Feeling warmth from sunlight
- Standing near a bonfire
- Feeling heat from a furnace
- Using infrared heaters
- Thermal imaging cameras

In all these situations, energy reaches you through thermal radiation.

---

# Radiation Does Not Require Contact

Unlike conduction:

```text
Hot Object
    │
 Contact Required
    │
Cold Object
```

Thermal radiation can transfer energy without direct contact.

```text
Hot Furnace
     ~ ~ ~ ~ ~
 Electromagnetic Waves
     ~ ~ ~ ~ ~
        ↓
     Human Body
```

Energy travels through space from one object to another.

---

# Why Thermal Radiation Is Important

Radiation becomes increasingly important as temperature rises.

At low temperatures:

- Conduction and convection often dominate.

At high temperatures:

- Radiation may become the dominant mode of heat transfer.

This is why radiation analysis is essential in:

- Furnaces
- Boilers
- Fired heaters
- Combustion chambers
- Solar energy systems

---

# Engineering Perspective

When a process engineer looks at a hot piece of equipment, they should immediately think:

- The equipment is losing heat through radiation.
- Nearby equipment may absorb some of that radiation.
- Personnel may be exposed to thermal radiation.
- Insulation may be required to reduce radiation losses.

Radiation is continuously occurring throughout industrial plants, even when it is invisible to the human eye.

# Key Takeaways

- Thermal radiation is electromagnetic energy emitted due to temperature.
- Every object above 0 K emits thermal radiation.
- Radiation does not require physical contact.
- Radiation does not require a material medium.
- Human bodies, steam pipes, reactors, and furnaces all emit thermal radiation.
- Radiation becomes increasingly important at high temperatures.

---

# Summary

Thermal radiation is the emission of electromagnetic energy by matter due to its temperature. Every object above absolute zero continuously emits radiation because of the motion of atoms, molecules, and electrons within the material. Radiation is unique because it can transfer energy without physical contact and without a material medium. Human bodies, steam pipes, reactors, and furnaces all emit thermal radiation, making it an essential mode of heat transfer in both daily life and industrial applications.
