# View Factor

<TopicRoadmap />


## Does Geometry Affect Radiation Heat Transfer?

Consider two situations:

### Case 1

```text
██████████
Surface 1

██████████
Surface 2
```

Large surfaces directly facing each other.

---

### Case 2

```text
██████████

          ██████████
```

The same surfaces, but positioned differently.

Will the radiation heat transfer be the same?

**No.**

Even if temperature and emissivity remain unchanged, geometry affects how much radiation reaches the other surface.

This geometric effect is described by the **View Factor**.

---

# What Is View Factor?

The View Factor represents the fraction of radiation leaving one surface that directly reaches another surface.

It is usually written as:

$$
F_{12}
$$

Where:

- Surface 1 is the emitting surface.
- Surface 2 is the receiving surface.

---

# Physical Meaning

If:

$$
F_{12}=1
$$

All radiation leaving Surface 1 reaches Surface 2.

---

If:

$$
F_{12}=0
$$

No radiation leaving Surface 1 reaches Surface 2.

---

If:

$$
F_{12}=0.5
$$

Only 50% of the radiation leaving Surface 1 reaches Surface 2.

---

# Visualization

```text
Surface 1

████████

↘ ↓ ↙
↘ ↓ ↙

████████

Surface 2
```

Most radiation leaving Surface 1 reaches Surface 2.

Therefore:

$$
F_{12}
\approx
1
$$

---

# Example: Parallel Plates

Two large parallel plates facing each other.

```text
████████████
 Plate 1

████████████
 Plate 2
```

Almost all emitted radiation reaches the opposite plate.

Therefore:

$$
F_{12}
\approx
1
$$

---

# Example: Furnace Walls

Inside a furnace:

```text
 ┌──────────┐
 │          │
 │  Tube    │
 │          │
 └──────────┘
```

Radiation emitted by hot refractory walls reaches process tubes.

The amount received depends on:

- Wall geometry
- Tube position
- Distance between surfaces

View factors help engineers calculate this radiation exchange.

---

# Example: Tube Banks

In heat recovery equipment and fired heaters, tubes are often arranged in banks.

```text
O   O   O

O   O   O

O   O   O
```

Not every tube "sees" every other tube equally.

Some radiation is blocked by neighboring tubes.

Therefore geometry strongly influences heat transfer.

---

# Why View Factor Matters

Radiative heat transfer depends on:

- Temperature
- Emissivity
- Surface Area
- Geometry

View factor accounts for the geometric part of the problem.

Without view factors, accurate radiation calculations would not be possible.

---

# Engineering Applications

View factors are used in:

- Furnace design
- Fired heaters
- Boilers
- Radiation shields
- Spacecraft thermal analysis
- Heat treatment furnaces

They help determine how much radiation actually reaches a target surface.

---

# Engineering Perspective

When a process engineer looks inside a furnace, they should think:

- Which surfaces can "see" each other?
- How much radiation reaches the tubes?
- Are there obstructions blocking radiation?

These questions are answered using view factors.

# Key Takeaways

- Geometry affects radiation heat transfer.
- View Factor represents the fraction of radiation leaving one surface that reaches another surface.
- It is denoted by:

$$
F_{12}
$$

- Values range from 0 to 1.
- Parallel plates often have view factors close to 1.
- View factors are essential in furnace and radiation calculations.

---

# Summary

View Factor is a geometric parameter used in radiation heat transfer analysis. It represents the fraction of radiation leaving one surface that directly reaches another surface. Even when temperature and emissivity remain unchanged, geometry can significantly affect radiation exchange. View factors are widely used in furnace design, tube-bank analysis, radiation shields, and industrial thermal systems.
