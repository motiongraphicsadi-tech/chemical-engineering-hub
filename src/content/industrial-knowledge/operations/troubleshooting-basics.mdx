# Introduction

Industrial plants operate under complex process conditions involving:

- high temperatures
- high pressures
- hazardous chemicals
- continuous operation
- automated systems

During operation, problems can occur due to:

- equipment failure
- process instability
- utility failure
- instrumentation issues
- operator error

Troubleshooting is the systematic process of identifying, analyzing, and correcting operational problems.

# What is Troubleshooting?

Troubleshooting is the process of:

1. identifying a problem
2. analyzing possible causes
3. finding the root cause
4. applying corrective action

The goal is to restore normal and safe plant operation.

---

# Why is Troubleshooting Important?

Industrial problems may lead to:

- production loss
- equipment damage
- unsafe conditions
- energy wastage
- environmental hazards

Proper troubleshooting helps industries:

- reduce downtime
- improve reliability
- maintain safety
- stabilize operation

# Basic Troubleshooting Steps

## Step 1 — Identify the Problem

Clearly understand:

- what happened
- when it happened
- where it occurred

---

## Step 2 — Collect Information

Operators and engineers gather:

- DCS trends
- alarm history
- field observations
- maintenance reports

---

## Step 3 — Analyze Possible Causes

Possible causes are evaluated systematically.

---

## Step 4 — Find Root Cause

Identify the actual reason behind the problem.

---

## Step 5 — Apply Corrective Action

Corrective actions are implemented safely.

---

## Step 6 — Monitor Results

Verify that the issue has been resolved properly.

---

# Root Cause Analysis (RCA)

Root Cause Analysis helps identify the main cause of a problem instead of only treating symptoms.

## Common RCA Methods

- 5 Why Analysis
- Fishbone Diagram
- Fault Tree Analysis

---

# Example of Troubleshooting

## Problem

Pump discharge pressure drops suddenly.

## Possible Causes

- suction blockage
- low tank level
- valve issue
- pump damage

## Corrective Action

Inspect suction line and restore proper flow.

# Troubleshooting Using DCS

Modern plants use DCS systems for troubleshooting.

# Safety Considerations

Troubleshooting must always prioritize safety.

## Common Risks

- toxic leakage
- high pressure release

- PPE usage
- permit systems
- lockout-tagout

<WarningBox>
