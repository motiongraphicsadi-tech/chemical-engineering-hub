# Introduction

Reactors are one of the most important equipment used in chemical and process industries.

A reactor is a vessel where chemical reactions occur under controlled conditions.

Reactors are used to produce:

- chemicals
- fuels
- polymers
- fertilizers
- pharmaceuticals

Industrial reactors operate under controlled:

- temperature
- pressure
- flow conditions
- mixing conditions

# What is a Reactor?

A reactor is industrial equipment designed to carry out chemical reactions safely and efficiently.

Inside the reactor:

- reactants interact
- chemical conversion occurs
- products are formed

Reactors are designed to maximize:

- conversion
- selectivity
- yield
- safety

---

# Why are Reactors Important?

Reactors are essential because they perform the actual chemical transformation process.

Industries use reactors for:

- chemical manufacturing
- polymer production
- fuel processing
- fertilizer production
- pharmaceutical synthesis

# Basic Working Principle

Reactants enter the reactor and undergo chemical reactions under controlled operating conditions.

The reactor controls:

- temperature
- pressure
- residence time
- mixing
- catalyst contact

to achieve desired product formation.

# Main Types of Reactors

Industrial reactors are classified based on operation and flow pattern.

## Common Reactor Types

1. Batch Reactor
2. Continuous Stirred Tank Reactor (CSTR)
3. Plug Flow Reactor (PFR)
4. Packed Bed Reactor

---

# Batch Reactor

Batch reactors process fixed quantities of reactants at one time.

## Working Principle

- reactants are charged
- reaction occurs
- products are removed after completion

## Advantages

- flexible operation
- suitable for small production

## Applications

- pharmaceuticals
- specialty chemicals

---

# Continuous Stirred Tank Reactor (CSTR)

CSTRs operate continuously with complete mixing.

## Characteristics

- continuous feed and product removal
- uniform composition inside reactor

## Advantages

- easy temperature control
- stable operation

## Applications

- liquid-phase reactions
- chemical manufacturing

<IndustrialInsight>
CSTRs are widely used in industries where good mixing and temperature control are important.
</IndustrialInsight>

---

# Plug Flow Reactor (PFR)

In PFRs, fluid moves through the reactor like plugs with minimal back mixing.

## Characteristics

- concentration changes along reactor length
- high conversion efficiency

## Applications

- petrochemical industries
- gas-phase reactions

---

# Packed Bed Reactor

Packed bed reactors contain solid catalyst particles packed inside the reactor.

## Applications

- catalytic reactions
- ammonia synthesis
- petroleum refining

# Important Reactor Parameters

## Temperature

Strongly affects reaction rate and selectivity.

---

## Pressure

Important for gas-phase reactions.

---

## Residence Time

Time reactants remain inside the reactor.

---

## Conversion

Percentage of reactants converted into products.

---

## Selectivity

Ability to produce desired product over undesired products.

---

# Heat Transfer in Reactors

Many chemical reactions generate or absorb heat.

## Exothermic Reactions

Release heat.

---

## Endothermic Reactions

Absorb heat.

Reactor temperature must be controlled carefully.

---

# Catalysts in Reactors

Catalysts increase reaction rate without being consumed.

## Benefits

- faster reactions
- lower energy requirement
- improved selectivity

---

# Reactor Operation and Monitoring

Operators monitor:

- temperature
- pressure
- flow rates
- conversion
- product quality

DCS systems continuously monitor reactor performance.

---

# Common Operational Problems

## Hot Spots

Localized high-temperature regions.

---

## Poor Mixing

Causes non-uniform reaction conditions.

---

## Catalyst Deactivation

Reduces reactor performance.

---

## Fouling

Deposits reduce efficiency.

<WarningBox>
Poor reactor temperature control can lead to runaway reactions, explosions, and major industrial accidents.
</WarningBox>

---

# Runaway Reactions

Runaway reactions occur when heat generation exceeds heat removal.

## Effects

- rapid temperature rise
- pressure increase
- equipment damage
- explosion risk

---

# Reactor Safety Considerations

Reactors operate under hazardous conditions.

## Common Risks

- high pressure
- toxic chemicals
- thermal runaway
- fire hazards

## Important Safety Practices

- pressure relief systems
- temperature monitoring
- emergency shutdown systems
- proper PPE usage
