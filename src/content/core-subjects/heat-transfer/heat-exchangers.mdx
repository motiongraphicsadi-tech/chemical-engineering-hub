## What is a Heat Exchanger?

A heat exchanger is a device used to transfer heat from one fluid to another without direct mixing of the fluids.

Heat exchangers are among the most important pieces of equipment in chemical plants because they help:

- recover energy
- reduce utility costs
- improve process efficiency
- control process temperatures

<IndustrialInsight>
Heat exchangers directly affect plant profitability because poor heat transfer increases steam usage, cooling water demand, and operational costs.
</IndustrialInsight>

---

## Basic Principle

Heat naturally flows:

- from high temperature fluid
- to low temperature fluid

The exchanger wall separates the fluids while allowing thermal energy transfer.

---

## Common Types of Heat Exchangers

### Shell and Tube Heat Exchanger

Most widely used industrial exchanger.

Features:
- tubes carry one fluid
- shell carries another fluid
- strong mechanical design
- suitable for high pressure operation

### Plate Heat Exchanger

Uses thin metal plates to increase heat transfer area.

Advantages:
- compact
- high efficiency
- easy maintenance

### Double Pipe Heat Exchanger

Simple exchanger where one pipe flows inside another.

Used for:
- small duties
- pilot plants
- low flow applications

## Heat Exchanger Equation

Overall heat transfer in exchangers is commonly expressed as:

<EquationBlock
  title="Heat Exchanger Equation"
  equation="Q = UAΔT"
  description="Heat transfer rate depends on overall heat transfer coefficient, heat transfer area, and temperature difference."
/>

---

## Understanding the Equation

- **Q** → Heat transfer rate  
- **U** → Overall heat transfer coefficient  
- **A** → Heat transfer area  
- **ΔT** → Temperature difference

## Fouling

Over time, deposits form on exchanger surfaces.

This is called fouling.

Common fouling causes:
- scaling
- corrosion products
- biological growth
- suspended solids

Effects of fouling:
- lower heat transfer
- higher pressure drop
- increased energy consumption

<WarningBox>
Fouling is one of the biggest operational problems in industrial heat exchangers and significantly increases operating costs.
</WarningBox>

---

## Flow Arrangements

### Parallel Flow

Both fluids move in the same direction.

Characteristics:
- simple design
- lower efficiency

### Counter Flow

Fluids move in opposite directions.

Advantages:
- higher heat transfer efficiency
- better temperature approach

### Cross Flow

Fluids move perpendicular to each other.

Common in:
- air coolers
- radiators

## Industrial Applications

Heat exchangers are used in:

- refineries
- fertilizer plants
- power plants
- food processing
- pharmaceutical plants
- petrochemical industries

Applications include:
- heating
- cooling
- condensation
- evaporation
- heat recovery

<IndustrialInsight>
Modern plants use heat integration networks to recover waste heat using multiple heat exchangers, dramatically reducing utility costs.
</IndustrialInsight>

## Heat Exchanger Performance Factors

Important parameters affecting performance:

- fluid velocity
- fouling resistance
- thermal conductivity
- exchanger area
- flow arrangement
- turbulence level

## LMTD Concept

Heat exchanger calculations often use:

- Log Mean Temperature Difference (LMTD)

because temperature difference changes continuously along exchanger length.

<EquationBlock
  title="LMTD Equation"
  equation="ΔT_{lm} = (ΔT_1 - ΔT_2) / ln(ΔT_1 / ΔT_2)"
  description="LMTD represents the effective average temperature difference across the exchanger."
/>
