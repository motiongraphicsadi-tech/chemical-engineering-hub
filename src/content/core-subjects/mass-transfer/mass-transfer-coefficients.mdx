# Mass Transfer Coefficients Introduction

In industrial mass transfer operations, species move from one phase to another through an interface. Examples include:

- Gas absorption
- Distillation
- Liquid-liquid extraction
- Humidification
- Drying

Although diffusion is the actual transport mechanism, engineers often use **mass transfer coefficients** to simplify calculations and design equipment.

Mass transfer coefficients relate the mass transfer rate to the driving force and provide a practical way to analyze industrial processes.

They are among the most important concepts in mass transfer engineering because nearly all industrial design calculations involve them.

---

Mass transfer coefficients help engineers:

# Core Concept

The rate of mass transfer depends on:

- Diffusivity
- Concentration difference
- Fluid properties
- Hydrodynamic conditions

Rather than solving complex diffusion equations every time, engineers use a coefficient that combines these effects.

General form:

$$
N_A = k \times \text{Driving Force}
$$

Where:

- \(N_A\) = Mass transfer flux
- \(k\) = Mass transfer coefficient

The coefficient measures how easily mass transfer occurs.

Higher coefficient:

$$
k \uparrow
$$

means faster mass transfer.

---

# Classification and Types

Mass transfer coefficients are classified based on the phase considered.

## Individual Gas-Side Coefficient

Represents resistance in the gas phase.

Symbol:

$$
k_G
$$

or

$$
k_y
$$

Used when gas-phase resistance is significant.

---

## Individual Liquid-Side Coefficient

Represents resistance in the liquid phase.

Symbol:

$$
k_L
$$

or

$$
k_x
$$

Used when liquid-phase resistance dominates.

---

## Overall Gas-Side Coefficient

Combines all resistances into one coefficient.

Symbol:

$$
K_G
$$

or

$$
K_y
$$

---

## Overall Liquid-Side Coefficient

Combines all resistances into one coefficient.

Symbol:

$$
K_L
$$

or

$$
K_x
$$

---

# Working Principle

Mass transfer between phases generally occurs as follows:

1. Species move through the bulk gas.
2. Species diffuse through the gas film.
3. Species cross the interface.
4. Species diffuse through the liquid film.
5. Species enter the liquid bulk.

Each step contributes resistance.

Mass transfer coefficients account for these resistances and simplify calculations.

---

# Mathematical Definition

## Gas-Side Flux Equation

$$
N_A
=
k_G(P_{A,b}-P_{A,i})
$$

Where:

- \(P_{A,b}\) = Bulk gas partial pressure
- \(P_{A,i}\) = Interfacial partial pressure

---

## Liquid-Side Flux Equation

$$
N_A
=
k_L(C_{A,i}-C_{A,b})
$$

Where:

- \(C_{A,i}\) = Interfacial concentration
- \(C_{A,b}\) = Bulk liquid concentration

---

## Overall Gas-Side Equation

$$
N_A
=
K_G(P_{A,b}-P_A^*)
$$

---

## Overall Liquid-Side Equation

$$
N_A
=
K_L(C_A^*-C_{A,b})
$$

Where:

- * indicates equilibrium concentration.

# Derivation and Theory

According to Film Theory:

$$
N_A
=
k(C_b-C_i)
$$

For gas and liquid phases:

$$
N_A
=
k_G(P_{A,b}-P_{A,i})
$$

and

$$
N_A
=
k_L(C_{A,i}-C_{A,b})
$$

Since flux must be equal through both phases:

$$
N_{gas}
=
N_{liquid}
$$

Overall coefficients are introduced to eliminate the unknown interfacial concentrations.

## Resistance in Series

For gas basis:

$$
\frac{1}{K_G}
=
\frac{1}{k_G}
+
\frac{m}{k_L}
$$

---

## Resistance in Series (Liquid Basis)

$$
\frac{1}{K_L}
=
\frac{1}{k_L}
+
\frac{1}{mk_G}
$$

Where:

- \(m\) = Equilibrium line slope

# Solved Example

### Problem

A gas absorption process has:

$$
k_G = 0.02
\;mol/(m^2.s.atm)
$$

Bulk gas partial pressure:

$$
P_{A,b}=0.50
\;atm
$$

Interfacial pressure:

$$
P_{A,i}=0.30
\;atm
$$

Calculate flux.

### Given

- \(k_G=0.02\)
- \(P_{A,b}=0.50\;atm\)
- \(P_{A,i}=0.30\;atm\)

### Solution

Using:

$$
N_A
=
k_G(P_{A,b}-P_{A,i})
$$

Substituting:

$$
N_A
=
0.02(0.50-0.30)
$$

$$
N_A
=
0.004
\;mol/m^2.s
$$

### Final Answer

$$
N_A
=
0.004
\;mol/m^2.s
$$

# Summary

Mass transfer coefficients provide a practical way to analyze and design mass transfer operations. Individual coefficients describe transport resistance in individual phases, while overall coefficients combine multiple resistances into a single parameter.

These coefficients are widely used in absorbers, distillation columns, extractors, strippers, and drying systems. Understanding gas-side coefficients, liquid-side coefficients, overall coefficients, and resistance concepts is essential for mass transfer equipment design and industrial process optimization.
