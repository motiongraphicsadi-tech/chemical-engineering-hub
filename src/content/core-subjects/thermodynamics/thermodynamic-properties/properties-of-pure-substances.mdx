# Introduction

Thermodynamics relies heavily on the measurement and analysis of properties.

Properties describe the condition of a substance and help engineers determine how a system behaves during heating, cooling, compression, expansion, and phase change processes.

Common thermodynamic properties include:

- Temperature
- Pressure
- Volume
- Density
- Internal Energy
- Enthalpy
- Entropy

Understanding these properties is essential for solving engineering problems.

---

# Why It Matters

Every thermodynamic calculation involves properties.

Engineers use properties to:

- Analyze energy balances
- Design equipment
- Predict phase changes
- Calculate efficiencies
- Perform process simulations

Properties provide the language used to describe thermodynamic systems.

---

# Core Concept

A thermodynamic property is any measurable characteristic of a system.

The state of a system can be described using a set of properties.

For example:

$$
State = f(P,T,V)
$$

where:

- \(P\) = Pressure
- \(T\) = Temperature
- \(V\) = Volume

Once sufficient independent properties are known, all other properties can be determined.

---

# Working Principle

Thermodynamic properties are used to define the state of a substance.

The general procedure is:

1. Measure known properties.
2. Locate the thermodynamic state.
3. Determine unknown properties.
4. Analyze the process.

This approach forms the basis of thermodynamic calculations.

---

# Mathematical Definition

Specific properties are obtained by dividing an extensive property by mass.

General relationship:

$$
Specific\ Property
=
\frac{Extensive\ Property}{Mass}
$$

Examples include:

$$
v=\frac{V}{m}
$$

$$
u=\frac{U}{m}
$$

$$
h=\frac{H}{m}
$$

---

# Intensive Properties

Intensive properties do not depend on the amount of matter present.

Examples:

- Temperature
- Pressure
- Density
- Specific Volume

Characteristics:

- Independent of system size
- Remain unchanged when system mass changes

These properties describe the condition of the substance.

---

# Extensive Properties

Extensive properties depend on the amount of matter present.

Examples:

- Mass
- Volume
- Internal Energy
- Enthalpy
- Entropy

Characteristics:

- Depend on system size
- Increase with increasing mass

Extensive properties are additive.

---

# Specific Properties

Specific properties are extensive properties expressed per unit mass.

Examples:

$$
v=\frac{V}{m}
$$

Specific Volume

$$
u=\frac{U}{m}
$$

Specific Internal Energy

$$
h=\frac{H}{m}
$$

Specific Enthalpy

Specific properties are widely used in engineering calculations.

---

# State Variables

State variables define the condition of a thermodynamic system.

Common state variables include:

- Pressure
- Temperature
- Volume
- Density
- Internal Energy
- Enthalpy
- Entropy

A complete set of independent properties fully specifies the state.

---

# Temperature

Temperature indicates the thermal condition of a substance.

Units:

- Kelvin (K)
- Celsius (°C)
- Fahrenheit (°F)

Temperature determines the direction of heat transfer.

Heat naturally flows from:

Higher Temperature → Lower Temperature

---

# Pressure

Pressure is the normal force exerted per unit area.

Mathematically:

$$
P=\frac{F}{A}
$$

Units:

- Pascal (Pa)
- kPa
- MPa
- bar

Pressure is one of the most important thermodynamic properties.

---

# Volume

Volume represents the space occupied by a substance.

Units:

$$
m^3
$$

Volume changes during:

- Compression
- Expansion
- Phase change

Volume is an extensive property.

---

# Density

Density represents mass per unit volume.

$$
\rho=\frac{m}{V}
$$

Units:

$$
kg/m^3
$$

Density is an intensive property.

---

# Specific Volume

Specific volume is volume per unit mass.

$$
v=\frac{V}{m}
$$

Units:

$$
m^3/kg
$$

Relationship with density:

$$
v=\frac{1}{\rho}
$$

---

# Internal Energy

Internal energy represents the total microscopic energy stored within a substance.

Symbol:

$$
U
$$

Specific internal energy:

$$
u=\frac{U}{m}
$$

Internal energy changes during heating, cooling, and phase change.

---

# Enthalpy

Enthalpy is a property widely used in flow processes.

Definition:

$$
H=U+PV
$$

Specific enthalpy:

$$
h=u+Pv
$$

Enthalpy is important for:

- Turbines
- Compressors
- Boilers
- Heat Exchangers

---

# Entropy

Entropy measures the degree of energy dispersal or disorder.

Symbol:

$$
S
$$

Specific entropy:

$$
s=\frac{S}{m}
$$

Entropy is central to the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

---

# Heat Capacity

Heat capacity indicates the amount of heat required to change temperature.

At constant pressure:

$$
C_p
$$

At constant volume:

$$
C_v
$$

Relationship:

$$
C_p > C_v
$$

for gases.

---

# Pure Substance Definition

A pure substance has uniform and fixed chemical composition throughout.

Examples:

- Water
- Nitrogen
- Oxygen
- Steam

A pure substance may exist in multiple phases while maintaining the same chemical composition.

---

# Phase Diagram

A phase diagram shows the regions where different phases exist.

Typical phases include:

- Solid
- Liquid
- Vapor

The diagram illustrates phase changes as pressure and temperature vary.

Phase diagrams are fundamental tools in thermodynamics.

---

# Triple Point

The triple point is the unique condition where:

- Solid
- Liquid
- Vapor

exist simultaneously in equilibrium.

For water:

$$
T = 0.01^\circ C
$$

$$
P = 0.611\;kPa
$$

Triple points are important thermodynamic reference states.

---

# Critical Point

The critical point represents the condition where liquid and vapor become indistinguishable.

Properties at the critical point:

$$
T=T_c
$$

$$
P=P_c
$$

Above the critical point:

- No distinct liquid phase
- No distinct vapor phase

Only a supercritical fluid exists.

---

# Saturated Liquid

A saturated liquid is a liquid that is about to vaporize.

Characteristics:

- Exists at saturation temperature
- Additional heat causes boiling

Symbol often used:

$$
f
$$

Example:

$$
h_f
$$

Saturated liquid enthalpy.

---

# Saturated Vapor

A saturated vapor is vapor that is about to condense.

Characteristics:

- Exists at saturation condition
- Small heat removal causes condensation

Symbol often used:

$$
g
$$

Example:

$$
h_g
$$

Saturated vapor enthalpy.

---

# Quality of Steam

Steam quality indicates the dryness of wet steam.

Definition:

$$
x=
\frac{Mass\ of\ Vapor}
{Total\ Mass}
$$

Range:

$$
0 \le x \le 1
$$

Where:

- \(x=0\) → Saturated Liquid
- \(x=1\) → Saturated Vapor

Steam quality is important in boilers and turbines.

# Solved Example

Water occupies:

$$
V=2\;m^3
$$

Mass:

$$
m=4\;kg
$$

Specific volume:

$$
v=\frac{V}{m}
$$

Substituting:

$$
v=\frac{2}{4}
$$

$$
v=0.5\;m^3/kg
$$

Therefore:

$$
Specific\ Volume=0.5\;m^3/kg
$$

---

# Common Mistakes

Students commonly:

- Confuse intensive and extensive properties
- Confuse density and specific volume
- Misinterpret steam quality
- Ignore phase regions
- Use incorrect property units

<WarningBox>
Steam quality applies only to wet vapor mixtures. It is not defined for compressed liquids or superheated vapors.
</WarningBox>

# Key Takeaways

- Thermodynamic properties describe the state of a system.
- Intensive properties do not depend on system size.
- Extensive properties depend on system size.
- Specific properties are expressed per unit mass.
- Enthalpy and entropy are key engineering properties.
- Phase diagrams describe phase behavior.
- Triple point and critical point are important thermodynamic states.
- Steam quality indicates the dryness of wet steam.
