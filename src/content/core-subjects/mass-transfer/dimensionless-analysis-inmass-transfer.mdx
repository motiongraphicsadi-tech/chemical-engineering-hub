# Dimensionless Analysis in Mass Transfer Introduction

Mass transfer processes depend on numerous variables including:

- Fluid velocity
- Diffusivity
- Density
- Viscosity
- Equipment dimensions
- Concentration gradients

Analyzing each variable separately becomes difficult for industrial systems.

Dimensionless analysis simplifies complex mass transfer problems by combining variables into dimensionless groups that reveal the dominant physical mechanisms governing transport.

The most important dimensionless numbers in mass transfer are:

- Reynolds Number (Re)
- Schmidt Number (Sc)
- Sherwood Number (Sh)

These numbers are widely used to develop empirical correlations and design industrial equipment such as absorbers, distillation columns, packed towers, and reactors.

---

# Why It Matters

Dimensionless analysis allows engineers to:

- Predict mass transfer coefficients
- Scale laboratory results to industrial systems
- Develop empirical correlations
- Compare different systems

Applications include:

- Gas absorption
- Distillation
- Drying
- Liquid extraction
- Packed towers
- Fluidized beds
- Membrane systems

Dimensionless numbers are among the most important tools in transport phenomena and process design.

# Core Concept

Dimensionless numbers are ratios of important physical forces or transport mechanisms.

They help determine:

- Flow behavior
- Relative importance of diffusion
- Convective transport effects
- Equipment performance

Instead of working with many variables, engineers use a few dimensionless groups that capture the essential physics.

These groups are obtained through:

- Buckingham Pi Theorem
- Dimensional analysis
- Experimental correlations

---

# Classification and Types

The major dimensionless groups used in mass transfer are:

## Reynolds Number (Re)

Represents the ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces.

Determines flow regime.

---

## Schmidt Number (Sc)

Represents the ratio of momentum diffusivity to mass diffusivity.

Compares momentum transfer and mass transfer.

---

## Sherwood Number (Sh)

Represents the ratio of convective mass transfer to molecular diffusion.

Used to calculate mass transfer coefficients.

---

# Working Principle

Dimensionless analysis works through the following process:

1. Identify important variables.
2. Form dimensionless groups.
3. Conduct experiments.
4. Develop correlations.
5. Predict mass transfer coefficients.
6. Apply correlations to industrial design.

The resulting correlations allow engineers to estimate transfer rates without solving complex differential equations.

---

# Mathematical Definition

# Reynolds Number

The Reynolds Number is:

$$
Re=\frac{\rho uL}{\mu}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| $\rho$ | Fluid Density |
| $u$ | Fluid Velocity |
| $L$ | Characteristic Length |
| $\mu$ | Dynamic Viscosity |

Interpretation:

- Low Re → Laminar Flow
- High Re → Turbulent Flow

---

# Schmidt Number

The Schmidt Number is:

$$
Sc = \frac{\mu}{\rho D_{AB}} = \frac{\nu}{D_{AB}}
$$

Where:

- $\nu$ = Kinematic Viscosity
- $D_{AB}$ = Diffusivity

Interpretation:

- Compares momentum diffusion and mass diffusion.

---

# Sherwood Number

The Sherwood Number is:

$$
Sh = \frac{kL}{D_{AB}}
$$

Where:

- $k$ = Mass Transfer Coefficient

Interpretation:

- Higher Sh indicates stronger convective mass transfer.

---

# Assumptions

Dimensionless correlations often assume:

- Steady-state operation
- Constant properties
- Uniform geometry
- No chemical reaction
- Fully developed flow
- Similar flow conditions

These assumptions improve applicability of correlations.

---

# Derivation and Theory

Dimensionless groups are obtained using dimensional analysis.

For mass transfer:

$$
k = f(\rho,\mu,D_{AB},u,L)
$$

Applying Buckingham Pi Theorem yields:

$$
Sh = f(Re,Sc)
$$

Thus:

- Reynolds Number represents flow effects.
- Schmidt Number represents diffusion effects.
- Sherwood Number represents transfer performance.

This forms the basis of most industrial mass transfer correlations.

---

# Key Relationships and Equations

## Reynolds Number

$$
Re = \frac{\rho uL}{\mu}
$$

---

## Schmidt Number

$$
Sc = \frac{\mu}{\rho D_{AB}}
$$

---

## Sherwood Number

$$
Sh = \frac{kL}{D_{AB}}
$$

---

## General Correlation

Many systems follow:

$$
Sh = a\,Re^b\,Sc^c
$$

Where:

- $a$, $b$, and $c$ are empirical constants.

---

## Example Correlation

For flow over flat plates:

$$
Sh = 0.664\,Re^{1/2}Sc^{1/3}
$$

This correlation is widely used in engineering calculations.

# Solved Example

### Problem

For a pipe flow system:

- Density:

$$
\rho = 1000\;kg/m^3
$$

- Velocity:

$$
u = 2\;m/s
$$

- Diameter:

$$
D = 0.05\;m
$$

- Viscosity:

$$
\mu = 0.001\;Pa\cdot s
$$

Calculate Reynolds Number.

### Given

- $\rho = 1000$
- $u = 2$
- $D = 0.05$
- $\mu = 0.001$

### Solution

Using:

$$
Re=\frac{\rho uD}{\mu}
$$

Substituting:

$$
Re=\frac{(1000)(2)(0.05)}{0.001}
$$

$$
Re=100000
$$

### Final Answer

$$
Re=1.0\times10^5
$$

The flow is highly turbulent.

# Summary

Dimensionless analysis provides a powerful framework for understanding and predicting mass transfer behavior. The Reynolds Number describes flow characteristics, the Schmidt Number compares momentum and mass diffusion, and the Sherwood Number quantifies convective mass transfer performance.

These dimensionless groups form the basis of empirical correlations used throughout chemical engineering for the design and analysis of absorbers, distillation columns, dryers, extractors, reactors, and other mass transfer equipment.
