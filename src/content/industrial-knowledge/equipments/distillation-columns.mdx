# What is Distillation?

Distillation is a separation process that separates components of a liquid mixture using differences in boiling point.

More volatile components vaporize more easily.

Less volatile components remain mostly in liquid form.

---

# What is a Distillation Column?

A distillation column is industrial equipment where vapor-liquid separation occurs continuously.

The column allows repeated:

- vaporization
- condensation

to improve separation efficiency.

---

# Why are Distillation Columns Important?

Industries use distillation columns for:

- product purification
- solvent recovery
- crude oil separation
- chemical production
- gas separation

Distillation helps industries achieve:

- high product purity
- continuous operation
- efficient separation

# Basic Working Principle

## Step 1

Feed enters the column.

---

## Step 2

Heat supplied at the bottom generates vapor.

---

## Step 3

Vapor rises upward through the column.

---

## Step 4

Liquid flows downward.

---

## Step 5

Mass transfer occurs between vapor and liquid.

More volatile components move upward.

Less volatile components move downward.

# Main Components of a Distillation Column

## Column Shell

Vertical vessel where separation occurs.

---

## Trays or Packing

Provide contact area between vapor and liquid.

---

## Reboiler

Provides heat at the bottom of the column.

---

## Condenser

Condenses vapor at the top.

---

## Reflux Drum

Stores condensed liquid before reflux.

---

## Feed Tray

Location where feed enters the column.

# Types of Distillation Columns

Industrial distillation columns are mainly classified into:

1. Tray Columns
2. Packed Columns

---

# Tray Columns

Tray columns use trays or plates for vapor-liquid contact.

## Common Tray Types

- sieve trays
- valve trays
- bubble cap trays

## Advantages

- suitable for large flow rates
- easier maintenance
- good operational flexibility

---

# Packed Columns

Packed columns use packing materials instead of trays.

## Packing Types

- random packing
- structured packing

## Advantages

- lower pressure drop
- higher efficiency
- suitable for vacuum operation

---

# Reflux in Distillation

Part of condensed liquid is returned to the column.

This is called:

# Reflux

Reflux improves separation efficiency.

---

# Reboiler Function

The reboiler provides heat energy required for vapor generation at the bottom of the column.

Common heating sources include:

- steam
- hot oil
- furnace heating

---

# Condenser Function

The condenser removes heat from overhead vapor and converts it into liquid.

The condensed liquid may become:

- reflux
- final product

---

# Distillation Terminology

## Overhead Product

More volatile product collected at the top.

---

## Bottom Product

Less volatile product collected at the bottom.

---

## Relative Volatility

Measure of separation difficulty between components.

# Important Operating Parameters

## Reflux Ratio

Ratio of returned liquid to collected distillate.

Higher reflux improves purity but increases energy consumption.

---

## Column Pressure

Affects boiling points and separation behavior.

---

## Temperature Profile

Indicates separation efficiency across the column.

---

# Common Operational Problems

## Flooding

Occurs when excessive vapor flow prevents proper liquid flow.

---

## Weeping

Liquid leaks through tray perforations due to low vapor flow.

---

## Fouling

Deposits reduce efficiency.

---

## Pressure Drop Increase

May indicate blockage or operational issues.

<WarningBox>
Poor distillation operation can reduce product purity and increase energy consumption significantly.
</WarningBox>
