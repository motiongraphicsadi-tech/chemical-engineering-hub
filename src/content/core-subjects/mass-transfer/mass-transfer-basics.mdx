# Introduction

## What is Mass Transfer?

Mass transfer is the movement of one or more chemical species from one location to another due to concentration differences or bulk fluid motion.

Mass transfer occurs whenever a component moves between:

- Two regions of different concentration
- Two phases (gas-liquid, liquid-liquid, solid-fluid)
- Different locations within a fluid

Examples include:

- Evaporation of water
- Absorption of ammonia in water
- Distillation of crude oil
- Drying of solids
- Oxygen transfer in bioreactors

Mass transfer is one of the three transport phenomena in chemical engineering, along with momentum transfer and heat transfer.

## Historical Background

The scientific study of diffusion began with the work of Adolf Fick in 1855, who proposed Fick's Law of Diffusion.

As chemical industries developed, engineers recognized that many separation and purification processes were governed by mass transfer principles. Today, mass transfer forms the foundation of numerous industrial operations including distillation, absorption, extraction, drying, crystallization, and membrane separations.

# Core Concept

Mass transfer occurs because systems naturally move toward equilibrium.

The driving force is usually:

$$
\text{Concentration Difference}
$$

A species moves from:

- High concentration region
- To low concentration region

Key terminology:

### Mass Transfer

Movement of species from one location to another.

### Diffusion

Mass transfer caused by molecular motion.

### Convection

Mass transfer caused by bulk fluid movement.

### Flux

Rate of mass transfer per unit area.

### Diffusivity

Property that measures how rapidly a species diffuses.

### Driving Force

The concentration difference causing mass transfer.

### Equilibrium

State where no net mass transfer occurs.

# Classification and Types

Mass transfer is generally classified into two major categories.

## Molecular Mass Transfer

Transfer occurs because of random molecular motion.

Examples:

- Perfume spreading through air
- Salt dissolving in water
- Gas diffusion through stagnant films

Governed by Fick's Law.

---

## Convective Mass Transfer

Transfer occurs due to bulk fluid motion.

Examples:

- Air drying wet clothes
- Gas absorption in packed towers
- Mixing in stirred tanks

Occurs simultaneously with fluid flow.

---

## Interphase Mass Transfer

Transfer between two phases.

Examples:

- Gas-liquid absorption
- Liquid-liquid extraction
- Drying operations

---

## Steady-State Mass Transfer

Mass transfer rate remains constant with time.

---

## Unsteady-State Mass Transfer

Mass transfer changes with time.

# Working Principle

Mass transfer occurs through the following sequence:

1. A concentration difference develops.
2. Molecules move from higher concentration regions.
3. Molecular diffusion transports species.
4. Bulk fluid motion may enhance transfer.
5. Concentrations gradually become uniform.
6. Equilibrium is eventually approached.

The larger the concentration difference, the higher the mass transfer rate.

# Mathematical Definition

The fundamental equation governing molecular diffusion is Fick's First Law:

$$
N_A = -D_{AB}\frac{dC_A}{dz}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Description | Unit |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| $N_A$ | Flux of component A | mol/m²·s |
| $D_{AB}$ | Diffusivity | m²/s |
| $C_A$ | Concentration of A | mol/m³ |
| $z$ | Direction of diffusion | m |

The negative sign indicates transfer from high concentration to low concentration.

# Assumptions

For simple diffusion analysis:

- Isothermal conditions
- Constant diffusivity
- No chemical reaction
- One-dimensional diffusion
- Homogeneous medium

These assumptions simplify mass transfer calculations.

# Derivation and Theory

Fick proposed that diffusion flux is proportional to the concentration gradient.

Mathematically:

$$
N_A \propto -\frac{dC_A}{dz}
$$

Introducing the proportionality constant \(D_{AB}\):

$$
N_A = -D_{AB}\frac{dC_A}{dz}
$$

For unsteady diffusion:

$$
\frac{\partial C_A}{\partial t}
=
D_{AB}
\frac{\partial^2 C_A}{\partial z^2}
$$

This equation is known as Fick's Second Law.

It describes how concentration changes with time and position.

# Key Relationships and Equations

## Fick's First Law

$$
N_A = -D_{AB}\frac{dC_A}{dz}
$$

## Fick's Second Law

$$
\frac{\partial C_A}{\partial t}
=
D_{AB}
\frac{\partial^2 C_A}{\partial z^2}
$$

## Convective Mass Transfer

$$
N_A = k_c(C_{A,b}-C_{A,s})
$$

Where:

- \(k_c\) = Mass Transfer Coefficient

## Total Flux

$$
N_A = N_{diffusion} + N_{convection}
$$

# Solved Example

### Problem

Oxygen diffuses through air with:

$$
D_{AB}=2\times10^{-5} \; m^2/s
$$

Concentration gradient:

$$
\frac{dC_A}{dz}
=
-1000 \; mol/m^4
$$

Calculate diffusion flux.

### Given

- \(D_{AB}=2\times10^{-5}\;m^2/s\)
- \(dC_A/dz=-1000\;mol/m^4\)

### Solution

Using Fick's First Law:

$$
N_A
=
-D_{AB}
\frac{dC_A}{dz}
$$

Substituting:

$$
N_A
=
-(2\times10^{-5})(-1000)
$$

$$
N_A
=
0.02
\; mol/m^2.s
$$

### Final Answer

$$
N_A = 0.02 \; mol/m^2.s
$$

# Summary

Mass transfer is the movement of chemical species from one location to another due to concentration differences or fluid motion. It occurs through diffusion and convection and forms the basis of many industrial separation and purification processes.

Fick's Laws provide the mathematical foundation for diffusion analysis, while convective transport explains mass transfer in flowing systems. Understanding mass transfer is essential for designing and operating distillation columns, absorbers, extractors, dryers, reactors, and many other chemical engineering systems.
