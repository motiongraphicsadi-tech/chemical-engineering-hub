# Electromagnetic Spectrum


<TopicRoadmap />

## Where Does Thermal Radiation Fit?

Thermal radiation is a form of electromagnetic radiation.

However, electromagnetic waves exist over a wide range of wavelengths known as the **electromagnetic spectrum**.

The spectrum includes:

- Ultraviolet (UV)
- Visible Light
- Infrared (IR)
- Microwaves
- Radio Waves

Each region differs primarily in wavelength and energy.

---

# Major Regions of the Spectrum

| Region | Wavelength (μm) |
|----------|----------|
| Ultraviolet (UV) | 0.01 – 0.4 |
| Visible Light | 0.4 – 0.7 |
| Infrared (IR) | 0.7 – 1000 |

# Ultraviolet Region

**Wavelength Range**

$$
0.01 - 0.4 \, \mu m
$$

Characteristics:

- Higher energy radiation
- Mostly emitted by very hot sources
- Can cause skin damage and material degradation

Examples:

- Sunlight (UV component)
- Welding arcs
- UV lamps

---

# Visible Region

**Wavelength Range**

$$
0.4 - 0.7 \, \mu m
$$

Characteristics:

- Detectable by the human eye
- Responsible for colors and vision

Examples:

- Sunlight
- Light bulbs
- Flames

---

# Infrared Region

**Wavelength Range**

$$
0.7 - 1000 \, \mu m
$$

Characteristics:

- Invisible to the human eye
- Associated with heat transfer
- Dominant region for thermal radiation

Examples:

- Human body radiation
- Steam pipes
- Reactor vessels
- Furnaces

---

# Why Infrared Is Important in Heat Transfer

Most engineering equipment operates at temperatures much lower than the Sun.

As a result, the radiation emitted by industrial equipment falls mainly within the infrared region.

Typical examples:

| Object | Peak Radiation Region |
|----------|----------|
| Human Body | Infrared |
| Steam Pipe | Infrared |
| Reactor Surface | Infrared |
| Furnace Tube | Infrared |

Therefore:

> Most thermal radiation encountered in engineering applications lies in the infrared region.

---

# Engineering Applications

Infrared radiation is used in:

- Infrared cameras
- Thermal imaging systems
- Furnace monitoring
- Equipment inspection
- Heat loss detection
- Predictive maintenance

Engineers often use infrared imaging to identify hot spots, insulation failures, and overheating equipment.

# Key Takeaways

- Electromagnetic radiation exists over a wide range of wavelengths.
- The major regions relevant to heat transfer are UV, Visible, and Infrared.
- Visible light occupies the range 0.4–0.7 μm.
- Infrared radiation occupies the range 0.7–1000 μm.
- Most thermal radiation from industrial equipment lies in the infrared region.
- Thermal imaging cameras detect infrared radiation emitted by hot surfaces.

---

# Summary

The electromagnetic spectrum consists of ultraviolet, visible, infrared, and other forms of electromagnetic radiation. For heat transfer applications, the infrared region is the most important because it contains the majority of thermal radiation emitted by engineering equipment such as steam pipes, reactors, and furnaces. Understanding the electromagnetic spectrum helps engineers connect radiation heat transfer with thermal imaging, infrared cameras, and industrial heat loss analysis.
