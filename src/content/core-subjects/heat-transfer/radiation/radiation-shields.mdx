# Radiation Shields

<TopicRoadmap />


## Why Does a Thermos Flask Have Shiny Walls?

A thermos flask can keep coffee hot for hours.

But how?

One important reason is the presence of **shiny reflective walls** inside the flask.

These shiny surfaces act as **radiation shields**, reducing heat transfer by thermal radiation.

---

# What Is a Radiation Shield?

A radiation shield is a surface placed between two bodies to reduce radiative heat transfer.

Typically, radiation shields are:

- Highly reflective
- Low emissivity
- Thin metallic surfaces

Their purpose is to reflect radiation rather than emit or absorb it.

---

# Low Emissivity Surfaces

Radiative heat transfer depends on emissivity.

$$
q \propto \varepsilon
$$

Therefore:

- High emissivity → High radiation heat transfer
- Low emissivity → Low radiation heat transfer

Polished metallic surfaces often have very low emissivity.

Examples:

| Material | Emissivity |
|-----------|-----------|
| Black Paint | ~0.95 |
| Oxidized Steel | ~0.80 |
| Polished Steel | ~0.15 |
| Polished Aluminum | ~0.05 |

Because polished aluminum emits very little radiation, it is widely used as a radiation shield.

---

# How Radiation Shields Work

Without a shield:

```text
Hot Surface
     ↓ Radiation
Cold Surface
```

Large radiative heat transfer occurs.

---

With a radiation shield:

```text
Hot Surface
     ↓

Radiation Shield

     ↓

Cold Surface
```

The shield reflects a significant portion of the radiation.

As a result:

- Less energy reaches the cold surface.
- Net heat transfer decreases.

---

# Reflective Barrier Concept

Radiation shields work like mirrors for thermal radiation.

```text
Incoming Radiation
        ↓

  Shiny Surface

↙ Reflected Radiation
```

Instead of absorbing and re-emitting radiation, the shield reflects much of the incoming energy.

This reduces heat transfer between the two surfaces.

---

# Heat Loss Reduction

Adding radiation shields can significantly reduce heat losses.

Benefits include:

- Energy savings
- Reduced fuel consumption
- Improved thermal efficiency
- Better temperature control

This becomes especially important in high-temperature and cryogenic systems.

---

# Application: Thermos Flask

A thermos flask uses:

- Vacuum insulation
- Reflective radiation shields

```text
Hot Coffee
     ↓
Vacuum Space
     ↓
Shiny Wall
     ↓
Outside
```

The vacuum reduces:

- Conduction
- Convection

The shiny wall reduces:

- Radiation

Together they greatly reduce heat loss.

---

# Application: Cryogenic Tanks

Cryogenic liquids such as liquid nitrogen and liquid oxygen are stored at extremely low temperatures.

Radiation heat gain from the surroundings can cause evaporation losses.

Radiation shields help:

- Minimize heat gain
- Reduce boil-off losses
- Improve storage efficiency

---

# Application: LNG Storage

Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) is stored at very low temperatures.

Radiation shields are used to:

- Reduce heat leakage
- Maintain low temperatures
- Improve energy efficiency

Even small reductions in heat gain can result in significant economic savings.

---

# Engineering Perspective

When engineers need to reduce radiation heat transfer, they often:

- Use polished metallic surfaces.
- Apply reflective coatings.
- Install multiple radiation shields.
- Select materials with low emissivity.

The goal is simple:

> Reflect radiation rather than emit it.

# Key Takeaways

- Radiation shields reduce radiative heat transfer.
- They are usually made from low-emissivity reflective materials.
- Polished aluminum is a common radiation shield material.
- Radiation shields work by reflecting thermal radiation.
- They reduce heat loss and heat gain.
- Thermos flasks, cryogenic tanks, and LNG storage systems rely heavily on radiation shields.

---

# Summary

Radiation shields are low-emissivity reflective surfaces used to reduce thermal radiation heat transfer. By reflecting radiation rather than absorbing and emitting it, they significantly reduce heat losses and heat gains. Radiation shields are widely used in thermos flasks, cryogenic storage systems, LNG tanks, and other applications where thermal efficiency is critical.
