Industrial plants operate under hazardous conditions involving:

- high pressure
- high temperature
- flammable chemicals
- toxic gases
- complex equipment systems

To improve safety and protect equipment, industries use:

- alarms
- interlocks
- emergency shutdown systems

These systems help operators identify dangerous conditions and automatically prevent accidents.

# What are Alarms?

Alarms are warning signals generated when process conditions move outside safe operating limits.

Alarms notify operators about:

- abnormal conditions
- equipment problems
- process instability
- safety risks

Alarms may appear as:

- flashing indicators
- audible sirens
- warning messages
- DCS notifications

---

# Purpose of Alarms

Industrial alarms help:

- prevent accidents
- protect equipment
- maintain process stability
- warn operators early
- reduce operational risk

---

# Common Industrial Alarms

## High Pressure Alarm

Generated when pressure exceeds safe limit.

---

## High Temperature Alarm

Generated when temperature becomes unsafe.

---

## Low Flow Alarm

Indicates insufficient process flow.

---

## High Level Alarm

Warns about excessive liquid level in vessels or tanks.

---

## Gas Detection Alarm

Activated when hazardous gases are detected.

# Alarm Priority Levels

Industrial alarms are classified by severity.

| Priority | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Low | Minor issue |
| Medium | Operational attention required |
| High | Serious operational problem |
| Critical | Immediate action required |

# Alarm Management

Proper alarm management is important because excessive alarms may confuse operators.

## Good Alarm Management Includes

- meaningful alarms
- proper prioritization
- operator training
- alarm rationalization

<WarningBox>
Too many unnecessary alarms can overload operators and delay response to critical emergencies.
</WarningBox>

---

# What are Interlocks?

Interlocks are automatic protection systems that prevent unsafe operations by automatically stopping or restricting equipment operation.

Unlike alarms, interlocks can take direct action without operator intervention.

---

# Purpose of Interlocks

Interlocks help:

- prevent equipment damage
- avoid unsafe operation
- reduce accident risk
- improve process safety

---

# Basic Working Principle of Interlocks

Interlocks continuously monitor process conditions.

If unsafe conditions occur:

- equipment may stop automatically
- valves may close
- alarms may activate
- emergency shutdown may begin

---

# Examples of Industrial Interlocks

## Pump Protection Interlock

Pump automatically trips if:

- low suction pressure occurs
- motor overheats
- vibration becomes excessive

---

## Boiler Interlock

Fuel supply stops automatically if:

- flame failure occurs
- pressure exceeds limit

---

## Reactor Safety Interlock

Feed valves close automatically if reactor temperature becomes too high.

---

# Interlocks vs Alarms

| Feature | Alarm | Interlock |
|---|---|---|
| Main Function | Warning | Automatic protection |
| Operator Action Required | Usually yes | Not always |
| Response Type | Notification | Direct control action |
| Safety Role | Alerts operators | Prevents unsafe operation |

---

# Emergency Shutdown System (ESD)

Industrial plants use Emergency Shutdown Systems for critical protection.

## ESD Functions

- stop equipment
- isolate process sections
- reduce pressure
- activate alarms
- prevent escalation

---

# Role of DCS in Alarms and Interlocks

Modern DCS systems handle:

- alarm generation
- alarm history
- interlock logic
- event recording
- operator notifications
