# Introduction

Pressure measurement is one of the most important operations in fluid mechanics and process industries.

Pressure measurements are required for:

- Process monitoring
- Equipment safety
- Flow measurement
- Level measurement
- Process control

Various devices have been developed to measure fluid pressure under different operating conditions.

Common pressure measurement devices include:

- Piezometer
- U-Tube Manometer
- Differential Manometer
- Inverted Manometer
- Industrial Pressure Gauges

Understanding these instruments is essential for chemical engineers and process engineers.

# Mathematical Definition

Pressure is defined as:

$$
P=\frac{F}{A}
$$

Where:

- \(P\) = Pressure
- \(F\) = Force
- \(A\) = Area

Hydrostatic pressure relationship:

$$
P=\rho gh
$$

Where:

- \(\rho\) = Density
- \(g\) = Gravitational Acceleration
- \(h\) = Fluid Height

Most pressure measurement devices are based on this principle.

---

# Piezometer

A piezometer is the simplest pressure measuring device.

It consists of:

- A vertical transparent tube
- Connected directly to the pressure point

Working Principle:

Pressure causes liquid to rise inside the tube.

Pressure is determined using:

$$
P=\rho gh
$$

Advantages:

- Simple construction
- Low cost
- Easy operation

Limitations:

- Suitable only for liquids
- Cannot measure vacuum pressure
- Not suitable for high pressures

Applications:

- Water systems
- Laboratory experiments
- Hydraulic studies

---

# U-Tube Manometer

A U-tube manometer consists of:

- U-shaped tube
- Filled with manometric fluid

Common manometric fluids:

- Mercury
- Water
- Oil

Working Principle:

Pressure difference causes displacement of liquid columns.

Pressure difference:

$$
\Delta P
=
\rho g h
$$

Where:

- \(h\) = Difference in liquid levels

Advantages:

- Accurate
- Simple
- No moving parts

Applications:

- Laboratory pressure measurement
- Gas pressure measurement
- Calibration work

---

# Differential Manometer

A differential manometer measures pressure difference between two points.

Working Principle:

The pressure difference causes movement of the manometric liquid.

Pressure difference:

$$
P_1-P_2
=
\rho g h
$$

Applications:

- Pipe flow analysis
- Flow measurement systems
- Pressure drop calculations

Advantages:

- Direct measurement of pressure difference
- High accuracy

Differential manometers are widely used in fluid mechanics experiments.

---

# Inverted Manometer

An inverted manometer contains a light manometric fluid.

Examples:

- Air
- Light oil

It is particularly useful for measuring:

- Small pressure differences
- Pressure differences between liquids

Advantages:

- High sensitivity
- Suitable for low differential pressures

Applications:

- Liquid flow systems
- Laboratory measurements
- Sensitive pressure measurements

# Pressure Measurement Techniques

Pressure may be measured using various methods.

### Hydrostatic Methods

Based on liquid column height.

Examples:

- Piezometer
- Manometer

---

### Mechanical Methods

Based on elastic deformation.

Examples:

- Bourdon Gauge
- Diaphragm Gauge
- Bellows Gauge

---

### Electronic Methods

Based on electrical signals.

Examples:

- Pressure Transmitters
- Piezoelectric Sensors
- Strain Gauge Sensors

These methods are commonly used in modern process industries.

# Industrial Pressure Measurement

Modern industries use advanced pressure instruments.

Common devices include:

### Bourdon Tube Gauge

Most widely used industrial pressure gauge.

Applications:

- Boilers
- Compressors
- Process pipelines

---

### Diaphragm Pressure Gauge

Used for:

- Corrosive fluids
- Slurries
- Low-pressure measurements

---

### Pressure Transmitters

Convert pressure into electrical signals.

Output signals:

- 4–20 mA
- Digital signals

Used in:

- Distributed Control Systems (DCS)
- PLC systems
- Industrial automation

# Factors Affecting It

Pressure measurement accuracy depends on:

### Temperature

Affects fluid properties and sensor calibration.

### Instrument Calibration

Poor calibration causes measurement errors.

### Vibration

May affect mechanical gauges.

### Installation Position

Incorrect installation may produce false readings.
