# Introduction

In industrial operations, many potentially serious incidents occur but do not result in injury, damage, or environmental impact due to luck, timing, or existing safeguards.

These events are known as **near misses**.

A near miss is an unplanned event that had the potential to cause injury, equipment damage, environmental release, or production loss but did not actually result in those consequences.

Near miss investigation is the systematic process of analyzing these events to understand what happened, why it happened, and how similar incidents can be prevented in the future.

<IndustrialInsight>
Many major industrial accidents were preceded by multiple near misses that were either ignored or never investigated.
</IndustrialInsight>

---

# Why It Matters

Near misses provide organizations with a unique opportunity.

Unlike accidents, near misses allow engineers to learn valuable safety lessons without experiencing actual harm.

Proper investigation helps identify:

- hidden hazards
- weak safety barriers
- procedural deficiencies
- equipment reliability issues
- human factor problems

Organizations that actively investigate near misses often prevent future incidents before they occur.

---

# Core Concept

The fundamental idea behind near miss investigation is:

> Treat every near miss as a free lesson before a real accident happens.

The focus is not on blaming individuals.

Instead, investigators seek to understand:

- what happened
- why it happened
- what barriers failed
- how recurrence can be prevented

Every near miss is viewed as an opportunity for improvement.

---

# Main Elements

A near miss investigation contains several important elements.

## Event Description

A factual description of the near miss.

Examples:

- small chemical leak
- temporary overpressure
- dropped object
- incorrect valve alignment

---

## Potential Consequences

Analysis of what could have happened if circumstances were slightly different.

Examples:

- injury
- fire
- explosion
- environmental release

---

## Immediate Causes

Direct causes that triggered the event.

Examples:

- equipment malfunction
- procedural error
- operator mistake

---

## Root Causes

Underlying system weaknesses.

Examples:

- inadequate training
- poor maintenance
- unclear procedures
- weak supervision

---

## Existing Barriers

Safety measures that prevented the event from becoming an accident.

Examples:

- alarms
- interlocks
- emergency shutdown systems
- operator intervention

---

## Corrective Actions

Actions taken to reduce future risk.

Examples:

- training updates
- procedure improvements
- equipment modifications

---

# Methodology

A structured near miss investigation generally follows these steps.

## Step 1

Report the near miss immediately.

---

## Step 2

Secure the area if necessary.

---

## Step 3

Collect evidence and facts.

---

## Step 4

Interview personnel involved.

---

## Step 5

Identify immediate causes.

---

## Step 6

Determine root causes.

---

## Step 7

Evaluate barrier effectiveness.

---

## Step 8

Develop corrective actions.

---

## Step 9

Share lessons learned.

# Industrial Example

Consider a centrifugal pump transferring a flammable solvent.

## Event

A small leak develops from the mechanical seal.

---

## Observation

The leak is discovered during routine inspection before ignition occurs.

---

## Potential Consequences

If the leak continued:

- fire
- explosion
- production shutdown

could have occurred.

---

## Investigation Findings

The investigation identifies:

- seal wear
- delayed replacement
- insufficient inspection frequency

---

## Existing Barrier

Routine operator inspection successfully detected the problem.

---

## Corrective Actions

- improve maintenance schedule
- revise inspection checklist
- increase monitoring frequency

---

## Lesson Learned

Early detection prevented a potentially serious process safety incident.

# Relationship with the Heinrich Triangle

Near miss investigations are often associated with the Heinrich Safety Pyramid.

The concept suggests that:

- many unsafe acts
- numerous near misses

exist beneath every major accident.

By addressing near misses, organizations reduce the probability of severe incidents occurring in the future.

---

# Root Cause Analysis

Near miss investigations frequently use root cause analysis techniques.

The objective is to move beyond:

"What happened?"

and understand:

"Why did it happen?"

Common root causes include:

- maintenance deficiencies
- design weaknesses
- communication failures
- procedural gaps
- training deficiencies

---

# Safety Culture and Reporting

A strong safety culture encourages employees to report near misses without fear of blame.

Successful organizations promote:

- open communication
- learning mindset
- continuous improvement
- proactive reporting

<WarningBox>
If employees are afraid to report near misses, valuable safety information may be lost and future accidents may occur.
</WarningBox>

# Engineering Perspective

Chemical engineers play an important role in near miss investigations.

They help:

- analyze technical causes
- evaluate equipment performance
- identify process hazards
- recommend corrective actions
- improve system reliability

Engineers often view near misses as early warning signals that reveal weaknesses before a serious incident occurs.

<IndustrialInsight>
Many process safety improvements originate from lessons learned through near miss investigations rather than actual accidents.
</IndustrialInsight>

# Key Takeaways

- A near miss is an event that could have caused harm but did not.
- Near misses provide valuable learning opportunities.
- Investigation focuses on causes and prevention, not blame.
- Root cause analysis helps identify underlying weaknesses.
- Existing barriers should be evaluated during investigations.
- Strong reporting culture improves organizational safety.

# Summary

Near miss investigation is a proactive safety management practice used to identify hazards and weaknesses before serious incidents occur. By analyzing events that could have caused harm, organizations gain valuable insights into equipment reliability, procedural effectiveness, and safety barrier performance.

A strong near miss reporting and investigation program improves process safety, strengthens organizational learning, and helps prevent future accidents. For chemical engineers, understanding near miss investigation is essential for building safer and more reliable industrial operations.
