# Introduction

Mass Transfer is one of the three fundamental transport phenomena in chemical engineering, alongside Fluid Mechanics and Heat Transfer.

It deals with the movement of chemical species from one location to another due to concentration differences, pressure differences, phase interactions, or chemical reactions.

Mass transfer governs almost every separation and purification process used in modern industries. Whether producing high-purity chemicals, refining crude oil, treating wastewater, manufacturing pharmaceuticals, or capturing carbon dioxide, mass transfer principles are at the core of the operation.

This subject provides the scientific and engineering foundation required to design and analyze separation equipment such as absorbers, distillation columns, extractors, dryers, crystallizers, adsorption beds, and membrane systems.

---

# Why Mass Transfer Matters

Mass transfer is responsible for:

- Product purification
- Chemical separation
- Pollution control
- Water treatment
- Pharmaceutical manufacturing
- Petrochemical processing
- Food production
- Energy and environmental systems

In many chemical plants, separation processes consume a significant fraction of total operating costs and energy consumption. Understanding mass transfer helps engineers improve efficiency, reduce costs, and optimize plant performance.
