# Introduction

Fluid Mechanics is the branch of science and engineering that studies the behavior of fluids at rest and in motion.

A fluid may be:

- Liquid
- Gas

Unlike solids, fluids continuously deform when subjected to shear stress.

Fluid mechanics helps engineers understand:

- Fluid flow through pipes
- Pump operation
- Mixing systems
- Transportation of liquids and gases
- Process equipment performance

It is one of the fundamental subjects in chemical engineering because almost every industrial process involves fluid handling.

---

# Why It Matters

Chemical plants continuously transport:

- Water
- Steam
- Oil
- Natural Gas
- Chemicals
- Slurries

Understanding fluid mechanics allows engineers to:

- Design piping systems
- Select pumps and compressors
- Estimate pressure drops
- Analyze flow behavior
- Optimize energy consumption

Without fluid mechanics, process industries could not efficiently transport raw materials and products.

# Definition

Fluid Mechanics is the study of fluids and the forces acting on them.

It is generally divided into:

### Fluid Statics

Study of fluids at rest.

Applications:

- Storage tanks
- Hydraulic systems
- Pressure measurement

### Fluid Dynamics

Study of fluids in motion.

Applications:

- Pipe flow
- Pumps
- Compressors
- Reactors

Together these fields form the foundation of fluid engineering.

# Physical Significance

Fluid mechanics explains how fluids behave under different operating conditions.

It helps engineers predict:

- Flow velocity
- Pressure changes
- Energy losses
- Flow patterns

Physical understanding of fluid behavior is essential for:

- Process design
- Equipment sizing
- Plant operation
- Troubleshooting

Every chemical plant depends on controlled fluid movement.

# Mathematical Definition

Fluid behavior is described using relationships between:

Pressure:

$$
P=\frac{F}{A}
$$

Density:

$$
\rho=\frac{m}{V}
$$

Specific Weight:

$$
\gamma=\rho g
$$

Specific Gravity:

$$
SG=\frac{\rho_{fluid}}{\rho_{water}}
$$

These quantities form the basis of fluid mechanics calculations.

---

# Classification of Fluids

Fluids may be classified based on their flow behavior.

### Ideal Fluid

An ideal fluid:

- Has no viscosity
- Is incompressible

Characteristics:

$$
\mu = 0
$$

Ideal fluids are theoretical and do not exist in reality.

---

### Real Fluid

Real fluids possess viscosity.

Examples:

- Water
- Air
- Oil
- Steam

All industrial fluids are real fluids.

---

### Newtonian Fluid

A Newtonian fluid follows:

$$
\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}
$$

Examples:

- Water
- Air
- Alcohol

Viscosity remains constant regardless of shear rate.

---

### Non-Newtonian Fluid

A non-Newtonian fluid does not obey Newton's law of viscosity.

Examples:

- Paint
- Toothpaste
- Polymer solutions
- Slurries

Viscosity changes with shear rate.

---

### Compressible Fluid

Density changes significantly with pressure.

Examples:

- Air
- Natural Gas
- Steam

---

### Incompressible Fluid

Density remains nearly constant.

Examples:

- Water
- Most liquids

---

# Difference Between Solids and Fluids

| Property | Solid | Fluid |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Shape | Fixed | No Fixed Shape |
| Shear Stress | Resists | Continuously Deforms |
| Volume | Fixed | Liquid: Fixed, Gas: Variable |
| Flow | Does Not Flow | Flows Easily |

The inability of fluids to resist shear stress distinguishes them from solids.

---

# Continuum Hypothesis

Fluid mechanics assumes fluids are continuous media.

This assumption is called the Continuum Hypothesis.

Instead of considering individual molecules, fluid properties are assumed to vary continuously.

Properties such as:

- Pressure
- Density
- Velocity
- Temperature

are treated as continuous functions.

This simplification makes engineering calculations possible.

# Basic Fluid Mechanics Terminology

### Density

Mass per unit volume.

$$
\rho=\frac{m}{V}
$$

Unit:

$$
kg/m^3
$$

---

### Specific Volume

Volume occupied by unit mass.

$$
v=\frac{V}{m}
$$

Unit:

$$
m^3/kg
$$

---

### Pressure

Force acting per unit area.

$$
P=\frac{F}{A}
$$

Unit:

$$
Pa
$$

---

### Viscosity

Resistance to fluid flow.

Dynamic viscosity:

$$
\mu
$$

Unit:

$$
Pa.s
$$

---

### Velocity

Rate of fluid movement.

$$
V=\frac{dx}{dt}
$$

Unit:

$$
m/s
$$

---

### Flow Rate

Volumetric flow rate:

$$
Q=AV
$$

Where:

- \(A\) = Area
- \(V\) = Velocity

Unit:

$$
m^3/s
$$

# Factors Affecting It

Fluid behavior depends on:

### Density

Higher density influences momentum and pressure forces.

### Viscosity

Controls flow resistance and energy loss.

### Temperature

Changes density and viscosity.

### Pressure

Especially important for gases.

### Flow Velocity

Determines flow regime and pressure drop.

These factors significantly affect industrial fluid systems.

# Common Mistakes

Students often:

- Confuse density and specific gravity
- Mix up pressure and force
- Ignore unit consistency
- Assume all fluids are incompressible
- Confuse viscosity and density

<WarningBox>
Density measures mass per unit volume, while viscosity measures resistance to flow. These properties are fundamentally different.
</WarningBox>

# Summary

Fluid Mechanics is the study of fluids and their behavior under various conditions. It provides the foundation for understanding fluid transport, pressure variation, flow behavior, and energy losses in industrial systems.

Chemical engineers use fluid mechanics extensively in the design and operation of pipelines, pumps, compressors, reactors, heat exchangers, and separation equipment. A strong understanding of fluid mechanics is essential for solving real-world chemical engineering problems.
