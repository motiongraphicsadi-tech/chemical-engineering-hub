# Introduction

When an object is immersed in a fluid, the fluid exerts an upward force on the object.

This upward force is known as the **buoyant force**.

The concepts of buoyancy and floatation are important in fluid mechanics because they explain:

- Why ships float
- Why balloons rise
- How hydrometers work
- How submerged structures behave

These principles are widely applied in marine engineering, chemical engineering, petroleum industries, and process equipment design.

---

# Why It Matters

Many industrial systems involve objects that are:

- Fully submerged
- Partially submerged
- Floating on liquid surfaces

Engineers use buoyancy principles to:

- Design storage tanks
- Analyze floating roofs
- Design ships and barges
- Develop level measurement devices
- Study offshore structures

Understanding buoyancy ensures safe and stable operation of engineering systems.

---

# Definition

Buoyancy is the upward force exerted by a fluid on a body immersed in it.

Floatation occurs when the buoyant force balances the weight of the body.

These concepts are governed by **Archimedes' Principle**.

---

# Physical Significance

Buoyancy occurs because fluid pressure increases with depth.

The pressure acting on the bottom surface of an immersed object is greater than the pressure acting on the top surface.

This pressure difference creates a net upward force.

As a result:

- Light objects float.
- Heavy objects may sink.
- Some objects remain suspended.

Buoyancy is a direct consequence of hydrostatic pressure distribution.

---

# Mathematical Definition

Buoyant force is given by:

$$
F_B
=
\rho_f g V_d
$$

Where:

- \(F_B\) = Buoyant Force
- \(\rho_f\) = Fluid Density
- \(g\) = Gravitational Acceleration
- \(V_d\) = Volume of Fluid Displaced

The weight of the body is:

$$
W
=
mg
$$

A body floats when:

$$
F_B = W
$$

---

# Archimedes' Principle

Archimedes' Principle states:

> A body immersed wholly or partially in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced.

Mathematically:

$$
F_B
=
\rho_f g V_d
$$

This principle forms the foundation of buoyancy calculations.

Applications include:

- Ships
- Submarines
- Hydrometers
- Floating tanks

---

# Buoyant Force

Buoyant force acts vertically upward through a point called the **center of buoyancy**.

The magnitude depends on:

- Fluid density
- Gravitational acceleration
- Displaced volume

Key observations:

- Higher density fluids produce larger buoyant forces.
- Greater displacement increases buoyancy.

For a fully submerged object:

$$
V_d = V_{object}
$$

---

# Floatation

A body floats when its weight is balanced by the buoyant force.

Condition for floatation:

$$
F_B = W
$$

If:

$$
F_B > W
$$

the body rises.

If:

$$
F_B < W
$$

the body sinks.

For floating bodies:

Only part of the object remains submerged.

---

# Stability of Floating Bodies

A floating body should not only float but also remain stable.

Stability determines whether a body returns to its original position after being disturbed.

Three possibilities exist:

### Stable Equilibrium

The body returns to its original position.

### Neutral Equilibrium

The body remains in its new position.

### Unstable Equilibrium

The body continues to tilt and may overturn.

Stability is extremely important in ship and vessel design.

---

# Metacenter

The metacenter is the point about which a floating body oscillates when slightly tilted.

The distance between:

- Center of Gravity (G)
- Metacenter (M)

determines stability.

This distance is called:

$$
GM
$$

(Metacentric Height)

---

### Stable Body

$$
M > G
$$

Positive metacentric height.

---

### Neutral Stability

$$
M = G
$$

Zero metacentric height.

---

### Unstable Body

$$
M < G
$$

Negative metacentric height.

---

# Factors Affecting It

Several factors influence buoyancy and stability.

### Fluid Density

Higher density increases buoyant force.

Examples:

- Saltwater provides more buoyancy than freshwater.

### Object Density

Objects less dense than the fluid tend to float.

### Shape of Body

Shape influences displacement and stability.

### Center of Gravity

Lower center of gravity improves stability.

### Metacentric Height

Larger positive metacentric height increases stability.

# Common Mistakes

Students often:

- Confuse weight and buoyant force
- Use object density instead of fluid density
- Ignore displaced volume
- Misinterpret stability conditions
- Forget metacenter concepts

<WarningBox>
Buoyant force depends on the density of the fluid displaced, not the density of the object itself.
</WarningBox>

- Buoyant force equals the weight of displaced fluid.

- Stability depends on metacentric height.
- A positive metacentric height indicates stable equilibrium.

- Fluid density strongly influences buoyant force.

# Summary

Buoyancy and floatation are fundamental concepts in fluid mechanics that explain the behavior of immersed and floating bodies. Archimedes' Principle states that the buoyant force acting on a body equals the weight of the displaced fluid.

Engineers use these principles to design ships, submarines, offshore structures, storage tanks, and level measurement devices. Stability analysis through the metacenter and metacentric height ensures safe and reliable operation of floating systems.
