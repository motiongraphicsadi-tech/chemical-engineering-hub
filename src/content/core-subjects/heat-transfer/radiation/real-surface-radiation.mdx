# Real Surface Radiation

<TopicRoadmap />


## Why Don't Actual Surfaces Behave Like Black Bodies?

A black body is an ideal surface that:

- Absorbs all radiation
- Emits maximum possible radiation

However, real engineering materials are not ideal.

Actual surfaces:

- Reflect some radiation
- Absorb only part of the incident radiation
- Emit less radiation than a black body

Therefore, real surfaces do not behave like black bodies.

---

# Real Emissivity

The ability of a real surface to emit radiation is measured by its **emissivity**.

$$
\varepsilon =
\frac{\text{Actual Emission}}
{\text{Black Body Emission}}
$$

For real surfaces:

$$
0 < \varepsilon < 1
$$

For a black body:

$$
\varepsilon = 1
$$

The lower the emissivity, the less radiation the surface emits.

---

# Effect of Surface Finish

Surface condition strongly affects radiation emission.

## Rough Surface

- Higher emissivity
- Better emitter
- Better absorber

Examples:

- Oxidized steel
- Refractory brick
- Black paint

---

## Polished Surface

- Lower emissivity
- Poor emitter
- Strong reflector

Examples:

- Polished aluminum
- Polished stainless steel
- Mirror-like surfaces

# Effect of Material

Different materials have different emissivities.

| Material | Typical Emissivity |
|-----------|-----------|
| Black Paint | 0.95 |
| Refractory Brick | 0.85 – 0.95 |
| Oxidized Steel | 0.70 – 0.90 |
| Stainless Steel (Polished) | 0.10 – 0.30 |
| Aluminum (Polished) | 0.03 – 0.10 |

Material selection therefore has a major impact on radiation heat transfer.

---

# Why Engineers Care About Emissivity

Radiation heat transfer for a real surface is:

$$
q = \varepsilon \sigma A T^4
$$

This means two surfaces at the same temperature can emit vastly different amounts of radiation.

Higher emissivity:

- More radiation emitted
- Greater heat loss

Lower emissivity:

- Less radiation emitted
- Reduced heat loss

---

# Industrial Examples

## Furnace Refractory

High emissivity surfaces are preferred because they radiate heat efficiently to process tubes.

---

## Steam Pipes

Insulated pipes often use protective coatings that influence radiation losses.

---

## Radiation Shields

Highly polished metallic surfaces are used because of their low emissivity and high reflectivity.

---

## Cryogenic Tanks

Low-emissivity reflective surfaces reduce radiation heat gain from surroundings.

---

# Engineering Perspective

When evaluating radiation heat transfer, engineers must consider:

- Surface temperature
- Emissivity
- Surface roughness
- Material type
- Surface oxidation

Two pieces of equipment operating at the same temperature may have completely different radiation losses because of differences in surface properties.

# Key Takeaways

- Real surfaces do not behave as perfect black bodies.
- Real surfaces have emissivities less than 1.
- Surface finish significantly affects radiation emission.
- Rough and dark surfaces generally have higher emissivity.
- Polished metallic surfaces generally have lower emissivity.
- Material selection strongly influences radiation heat transfer.
- Real surface radiation is calculated using emissivity.

---

# Summary

Real engineering surfaces emit less radiation than a black body because their emissivity is less than one. Radiation behavior depends on material properties, surface finish, oxidation, and roughness. Dark, rough surfaces generally have high emissivity and emit more radiation, while polished metallic surfaces have low emissivity and emit less radiation. Understanding real surface radiation is essential for furnace design, insulation systems, radiation shields, and industrial energy efficiency calculations.
