# Introduction

Fluids do not always behave in the same manner when subjected to shear stress.

Some fluids exhibit a linear relationship between shear stress and velocity gradient, while others show complex behavior.

Based on this response, fluids are classified into:

- Newtonian Fluids
- Non-Newtonian Fluids

Understanding fluid behavior is essential for designing industrial equipment such as:

- Pumps
- Mixers
- Pipelines
- Extruders
- Reactors

Many industrial fluids such as polymer melts, slurries, paints, food products, and toothpaste exhibit non-Newtonian behavior.

---

# Why It Matters

Chemical engineers frequently handle fluids that do not behave like water or air.

Examples include:

- Polymer solutions
- Paints
- Ketchup
- Toothpaste
- Slurries
- Drilling muds

Understanding rheological behavior helps engineers:

- Design pumping systems
- Estimate pressure drops
- Optimize mixing operations
- Improve product quality
- Select suitable process equipment

Incorrect assumptions about fluid behavior can lead to major design errors.

# Definition

Fluids are classified according to how shear stress changes with velocity gradient.

### Newtonian Fluid

A fluid that obeys Newton's Law of Viscosity.

### Non-Newtonian Fluid

A fluid that does not obey Newton's Law of Viscosity.

The distinction is based on the relationship between shear stress and shear rate.

---

# Physical Significance

Fluid behavior determines:

- Flow resistance
- Pressure losses
- Mixing characteristics
- Energy requirements

Newtonian fluids have constant viscosity.

Non-Newtonian fluids have viscosity that changes with shear conditions.

Understanding these differences is critical for industrial process design.

---

# Mathematical Definition

For Newtonian fluids:

$$
\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}
$$

Where:

- \(\tau\) = Shear Stress
- \(\mu\) = Dynamic Viscosity
- \(du/dy\) = Velocity Gradient

The relationship is linear.

For non-Newtonian fluids:

$$
\tau \neq \mu \frac{du}{dy}
$$

Viscosity varies with shear conditions.

---

# Newtonian Fluids

Newtonian fluids obey Newton's Law of Viscosity.

Characteristics:

- Constant viscosity
- Linear shear stress relationship
- Predictable flow behavior

Examples:

- Water
- Air
- Alcohol
- Benzene
- Glycerol (within normal ranges)

Graphically:

Shear stress increases linearly with shear rate.

These fluids are easiest to analyze mathematically.

---

# Non-Newtonian Fluids

Non-Newtonian fluids do not exhibit constant viscosity.

Characteristics:

- Viscosity changes with shear rate
- Non-linear flow behavior
- More complex rheology

Examples:

- Paint
- Blood
- Toothpaste
- Polymer melts
- Slurries

Most industrial fluids are non-Newtonian.

---

# Bingham Plastic Fluids

Bingham plastics require a minimum shear stress before flow begins.

This minimum stress is called yield stress.

Mathematically:

$$
\tau = \tau_y + \mu_p \frac{du}{dy}
$$

Where:

- \(\tau_y\) = Yield Stress
- \(\mu_p\) = Plastic Viscosity

Examples:

- Toothpaste
- Drilling Mud
- Chocolate Paste

These fluids behave like solids until the yield stress is exceeded.

---

# Pseudoplastic Fluids

Pseudoplastic fluids are shear-thinning fluids.

As shear rate increases:

$$
\mu \downarrow
$$

Viscosity decreases.

Examples:

- Paint
- Blood
- Polymer Solutions
- Ketchup

Advantages:

- Easier pumping at higher flow rates
- Improved mixing

This is one of the most common non-Newtonian behaviors.

---

# Dilatant Fluids

Dilatant fluids are shear-thickening fluids.

As shear rate increases:

$$
\mu \uparrow
$$

Viscosity increases.

Examples:

- Cornstarch Suspensions
- Wet Sand
- Certain Ceramic Slurries

These fluids become more resistant to flow under high shear conditions.

---

# Rheological Behavior

Rheology is the study of fluid deformation and flow.

Rheological analysis helps engineers determine:

- Viscosity behavior
- Flow resistance
- Material handling requirements

Common rheological behaviors include:

- Newtonian
- Bingham Plastic
- Pseudoplastic
- Dilatant

Rheological testing is commonly performed using rheometers and viscometers.

---

# Factors Affecting It

Several factors influence fluid rheology.

### Temperature

Temperature changes viscosity significantly.

### Shear Rate

Important for non-Newtonian fluids.

### Composition

Mixture concentration affects flow behavior.

### Particle Content

Slurries become more complex as solids increase.

### Molecular Structure

Polymer chain length strongly influences viscosity.

These factors must be considered during process design.

# Common Mistakes

Students often:

- Assume all fluids are Newtonian
- Confuse viscosity with density
- Ignore yield stress effects
- Use Newtonian equations for non-Newtonian fluids
- Forget the effect of shear rate

<WarningBox>
Most industrial fluids are non-Newtonian. Always verify fluid behavior before selecting design equations.
</WarningBox>

# Summary

Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids differ in how they respond to shear stress. Newtonian fluids exhibit constant viscosity and obey Newton's Law of Viscosity, while non-Newtonian fluids display complex rheological behavior.

Understanding Bingham plastics, pseudoplastic fluids, and dilatant fluids is essential for chemical engineers working in polymer, food, pharmaceutical, petroleum, and process industries. Proper rheological analysis ensures efficient equipment design, reliable operation, and high product quality.
