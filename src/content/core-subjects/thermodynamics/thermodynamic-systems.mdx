# Introduction

Thermodynamics begins with the study of systems.

Before analyzing energy, heat, or work interactions, engineers must clearly define what portion of the universe is being studied.

A thermodynamic system may be:

- A reactor
- A boiler
- A compressor
- A turbine
- A heat exchanger
- An entire industrial plant

Proper system definition is the first step in every thermodynamic analysis.

---

# Why It Matters

All thermodynamic calculations depend on the correct selection of the system.

Engineers use system analysis to:

- Perform energy balances
- Analyze mass balances
- Design process equipment
- Predict process behavior
- Evaluate efficiency

Incorrect system definition often leads to incorrect engineering calculations.

---

# Core Concept

A thermodynamic system is a specified quantity of matter or a region in space selected for study.

Everything outside the system is called the surroundings.

Together:

$$
Universe
=
System + Surroundings
$$

Thermodynamic analysis focuses on interactions between the system and its surroundings.

---

# Working Principle

The analysis of thermodynamic systems generally follows these steps:

1. Define the system.
2. Identify the surroundings.
3. Specify the boundary.
4. Determine mass interactions.
5. Determine energy interactions.
6. Apply thermodynamic laws.

This approach forms the basis of all thermodynamic calculations.

---

# Mathematical Definition

The total energy of a system can be represented as:

$$
E = U + KE + PE
$$

Where:

- \(U\) = Internal Energy
- \(KE\) = Kinetic Energy
- \(PE\) = Potential Energy

Thermodynamic systems are analyzed by tracking changes in these energy forms.

---

# Definition of a System

A system is the portion of the universe selected for analysis.

Examples:

- Steam inside a boiler
- Gas inside a cylinder
- Fluid flowing through a turbine

The system is the primary focus of thermodynamic study.

---

# Surroundings

Everything external to the system is called the surroundings.

Examples:

- Atmosphere
- Equipment walls
- Utility systems
- Nearby equipment

The surroundings may exchange:

- Heat
- Work
- Mass

with the system depending on the system type.

---

# Boundary

The boundary separates the system from the surroundings.

Characteristics:

- Real or imaginary
- Fixed or movable
- Adiabatic or non-adiabatic

The boundary determines how mass and energy interactions occur.

---

# Universe

In thermodynamics:

$$
Universe
=
System + Surroundings
$$

The universe includes everything associated with the analysis.

This concept is frequently used in entropy and second law analysis.

---

# Types of Systems

Thermodynamic systems are classified according to how they interact with their surroundings.

Main classifications include:

- Open System
- Closed System
- Isolated System

---

# Open System

An open system allows both:

- Mass transfer
- Energy transfer

across its boundary.

Examples:

- Turbines
- Compressors
- Pumps
- Heat Exchangers

Mass continuously enters and leaves the system.

---

# Closed System

A closed system allows:

- Energy transfer

but does not allow:

- Mass transfer

across the boundary.

Example:

A sealed piston-cylinder device.

Mass remains constant while heat and work interactions may occur.

---

# Isolated System

An isolated system allows neither:

- Mass transfer
- Energy transfer

with the surroundings.

Ideal example:

$$
Q = 0
$$

and

$$
W = 0
$$

Perfect isolated systems do not exist in practice but are useful theoretical models.

---

# Homogeneous System

A homogeneous system contains only one phase and has uniform properties throughout.

Examples:

- Pure liquid water
- Pure steam
- Air in a room

Properties remain identical at every location.

---

# Heterogeneous System

A heterogeneous system contains multiple phases.

Examples:

- Ice and water
- Water and steam
- Liquid and vapor mixtures

Properties vary from one phase to another.

---

# Control Mass

A control mass is a fixed quantity of matter selected for analysis.

Characteristics:

- Mass remains constant
- No mass crosses boundary

Example:

Gas trapped inside a sealed cylinder.

Control mass analysis is commonly used in closed systems.

---

# Control Volume

A control volume is a fixed region in space through which mass may flow.

Characteristics:

- Mass may enter
- Mass may leave
- Energy may cross boundary

Examples:

- Turbine
- Pump
- Compressor
- Heat Exchanger

Most industrial equipment is analyzed as a control volume.

---

# State of a System

The state of a system describes its condition at a particular instant.

State properties include:

- Pressure
- Temperature
- Volume
- Density

A system is completely specified when sufficient independent properties are known.

---

# Equilibrium State

A system is in equilibrium when no driving forces exist for change.

Properties remain constant with time.

Equilibrium requires:

- Thermal Equilibrium
- Mechanical Equilibrium
- Phase Equilibrium
- Chemical Equilibrium

All must exist simultaneously.

---

# Thermal Equilibrium

Thermal equilibrium exists when:

$$
T_1 = T_2
$$

No temperature differences exist within the system.

Therefore:

$$
Heat\ Transfer = 0
$$

inside the system.

---

# Mechanical Equilibrium

Mechanical equilibrium exists when pressure is uniform throughout the system.

$$
P = Constant
$$

No unbalanced forces are present.

The system experiences no mechanical changes.

---

# Phase Equilibrium

Phase equilibrium exists when multiple phases coexist without net phase change.

Examples:

- Liquid water and steam
- Ice and water

At equilibrium:

$$
Rate\ of\ Evaporation
=
Rate\ of\ Condensation
$$

---

# Chemical Equilibrium

Chemical equilibrium exists when chemical reactions have reached a stable condition.

$$
Rate\ of\ Forward\ Reaction
=
Rate\ of\ Reverse\ Reaction
$$

The composition remains constant with time.

Chemical equilibrium is important in reactor design and reaction engineering.

---

# Industrial Applications

Thermodynamic system concepts are used in:

- Boilers
- Turbines
- Compressors
- Distillation Columns
- Heat Exchangers
- Reactors
- Refrigeration Systems

Engineers must define systems correctly before performing thermodynamic calculations.

---

# Advantages

System analysis helps engineers:

- Simplify complex processes
- Perform energy balances
- Analyze mass flow
- Design equipment
- Improve efficiency

It provides a structured approach to thermodynamic problem solving.

---

# Limitations

Thermodynamic system analysis:

- Does not predict reaction rates
- Does not determine equipment size directly
- Requires simplifying assumptions
- May ignore detailed molecular behavior

Additional engineering tools are often required.

---

# Engineering Perspective

Chemical engineers routinely define:

- Control Volumes
- Control Masses
- Open Systems
- Closed Systems

before solving engineering problems.

Most industrial equipment operates as open systems because mass continuously flows through them.

Understanding system boundaries is one of the most important skills in thermodynamics.

---

# Solved Example

A compressor receives air continuously and discharges compressed air continuously.

Mass enters:

$$
\dot m_{in}
$$

Mass leaves:

$$
\dot m_{out}
$$

The compressor also consumes work:

$$
W_{in}
$$

This system is classified as:

$$
Open\ System
$$

because both mass and energy cross the system boundary.

---

# Common Mistakes

Students commonly:

- Confuse open and closed systems
- Forget to define system boundaries
- Assume all systems are control volumes
- Confuse homogeneous and heterogeneous systems
- Ignore equilibrium requirements

<WarningBox>
A closed system allows energy transfer but does not allow mass transfer. Many students incorrectly assume that closed systems prohibit both.
</WarningBox>

---

# Key Takeaways

- A thermodynamic system is the portion selected for analysis.
- The surroundings include everything outside the system.
- The boundary separates the system from the surroundings.
- Open systems allow mass and energy transfer.
- Closed systems allow energy transfer but not mass transfer.
- Isolated systems allow neither mass nor energy transfer.
- Equilibrium requires thermal, mechanical, phase, and chemical equilibrium.
- Most industrial equipment is analyzed as a control volume.
