# Humidification, Drying and Crystallization Introduction

Humidification, drying, and crystallization are important mass transfer operations used extensively in chemical process industries.

These operations involve transfer of mass between phases and are essential for:

- Product purification
- Moisture control
- Solid production
- Material recovery
- Process optimization

Humidification deals with vapor transfer into gases, drying removes moisture from solids, and crystallization produces solid crystals from solutions or melts.

Together, these processes form a major part of industrial separation and product manufacturing operations.

---

# Why It Matters

These operations are important because they enable:

- Production of high-purity solids
- Moisture removal
- Air conditioning and cooling
- Pharmaceutical manufacturing
- Food processing
- Chemical production

Applications include:

- Cooling towers
- Spray dryers
- Crystallizers
- Salt production
- Sugar manufacturing
- Fertilizer production

Understanding these operations is essential for chemical engineers involved in separation and product processing.

---

# Core Concept

## Humidification

Humidification increases the moisture content of a gas by transferring vapor from a liquid to a gas phase.

---

## Drying

Drying removes moisture from wet solids through evaporation.

---

## Crystallization

Crystallization produces solid crystals from supersaturated solutions.

Each operation depends on mass transfer and phase equilibrium principles.

# Working Principle

## Humidification

1. Gas contacts liquid.
2. Water evaporates.
3. Moisture transfers to gas.
4. Humidity increases.
5. Gas approaches saturation.

---

## Drying

1. Wet solid contacts hot gas.
2. Moisture evaporates.
3. Vapor diffuses into gas stream.
4. Moisture content decreases.

---

## Crystallization

1. Supersaturated solution is created.
2. Crystal nuclei form.
3. Crystals grow.
4. Solid crystals are recovered.

---

# Mathematical Definition

## Humidity

Absolute humidity:

$$
Y
=
\frac{\text{Mass of Water Vapor}}
{\text{Mass of Dry Air}}
$$

Units:

$$
kg\;water/kg\;dry\;air
$$

---

## Relative Humidity

$$
RH
=
\frac{P_v}{P_v^{sat}}
\times100
$$

Where:

- \(P_v\) = Vapor partial pressure
- \(P_v^{sat}\) = Saturation vapor pressure

---

## Drying Rate

$$
R
=
-\frac{dM}{dt}
$$

Where:

- \(R\) = Drying rate
- \(M\) = Moisture content

---

## Supersaturation Ratio

$$
S
=
\frac{C}{C^*}
$$

Where:

- \(C\) = Actual concentration
- \(C^*\) = Saturation concentration

Crystallization requires:

$$
S>1
$$

# Drying Mechanism

Drying occurs in two stages:

## Constant Rate Period

Surface remains saturated.

Drying rate remains approximately constant.

---

## Falling Rate Period

Internal moisture diffusion controls drying.

Drying rate decreases continuously.

Most industrial drying time occurs in this period.

---

# Supersaturation

Supersaturation is the driving force for crystallization.

Defined as:

$$
S
=
\frac{C}{C^*}
$$

When:

$$
S=1
$$

Solution is saturated.

When:

$$
S>1
$$

Crystallization becomes possible.

---

# Crystal Growth

After nucleation:

1. Solute molecules diffuse to crystal surface.
2. Molecules integrate into crystal lattice.
3. Crystal size increases.

Growth rate depends on supersaturation.

# Solved Example

### Problem

A solution contains:

$$
C=120 \; g/L
$$

The saturation concentration is:

$$
C^*=100 \; g/L
$$

Calculate supersaturation ratio.

### Given

- \(C=120\;g/L\)
- \(C^*=100\;g/L\)

### Solution

Using:

$$
S=\frac{C}{C^*}
$$

Substituting:

$$
S=\frac{120}{100}
$$

$$
S=1.2
$$

### Final Answer

$$
S=1.2
$$

The solution is supersaturated and capable of crystallization.
