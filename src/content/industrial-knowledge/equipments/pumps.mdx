# What is a Pump?

A pump transfers fluid from one place to another by increasing fluid pressure and flow energy.

Main purposes include:

- Transfer liquid
- Increase pressure
- Circulation
- Dosing
- Cooling
- Feed systems
- Chemical injection

---

# Main Classification of Pumps

Industrial pumps are mainly classified into:

1. <span className="highlight">Dynamic Pumps</span>
2. <span className="highlight">Positive Displacement Pumps</span>

---

# Dynamic Pumps

Dynamic pumps continuously increase fluid velocity during operation.

Types include:

- Centrifugal pump
- Axial flow pump
- Mixed flow pump



## Centrifugal Pump Fundamentals

Centrifugal pumps use rotating impellers to increase fluid velocity and pressure.

Working principle:

- Impeller rotates
- Fluid gains velocity
- Casing converts velocity into pressure

Main components include:

<span className="highlight">Impeller, Casing, Shaft, Bearings, Mechanical seal, Coupling, Wear ring</span>

# Positive Displacement Pumps

Positive displacement pumps trap and mechanically push fixed fluid volumes.

Types include:

- Reciprocating pump
- Gear pump
- Screw pump
- Diaphragm pump
- Lobe pump
- Piston pump
- Peristaltic pump

Used for:

- High viscosity fluids
- Accurate dosing
- High pressure operation
- Low flow applications


## Reciprocating Pump Fundamentals

Reciprocating pumps use the back-and-forth motion of pistons or plungers to move fluids and generate high pressure.

Working principle:

- Piston moves backward and creates suction
- Fluid enters the cylinder through suction valve
- Piston moves forward and pushes fluid through discharge valve

Main components include:

<span className="highlight">Cylinder, Piston, Plunger, Suction valve, Discharge valve, Connecting rod, Crankshaft</span>

# Dynamic Pumps vs Positive Displacement Pumps

these are the basic difference

| Feature | Dynamic Pump | Positive Displacement Pump |
|---|---|---|
| Working Principle | Increases fluid velocity continuously | Traps and mechanically pushes fixed fluid volume |
| Flow Type | Continuous flow | Pulsating or fixed flow |
| Pressure Capability | Suitable for low to medium pressure | Suitable for high pressure |
| Flow Rate | High flow applications | Low flow applications |
| Viscosity Handling | Poor for highly viscous fluids | Good for viscous fluids |
| Most Common Type | Centrifugal pump | Gear pump, piston pump |
| Priming Requirement | Usually requires priming | Self-priming in many cases |
| Flow Control | Easily controlled using valves | Flow mostly depends on displacement |
| Efficiency at High Pressure | Lower | Higher |
| Typical Applications | Cooling water, circulation, utility systems | Dosing, oil transfer, chemical injection |
| Maintenance | Generally lower maintenance | More moving parts and maintenance |
| Industrial Use | Most common industrial pump type | Specialized industrial applications |

# Important Pump Terminology

## Flow Rate (Q)

Flow rate represents the quantity of liquid moved per unit time.

Common units:

- m³/hr
- LPM
- GPM

---

## Head (H)

Head represents energy added to the liquid by the pump.

Measured in meters.

Total Dynamic Head (TDH) includes:

- Static head
- Friction losses
- Pressure head
- Velocity head

---

## Pressure Relation

Pressure generated by the pump depends on fluid density, gravity, and head.

<EquationBlock
  title="Pressure and Head Relation"
  equation="P = ρgH"
  description="Pump pressure depends on density, gravity, and developed head."
/>

---

## Pump Efficiency

Pump efficiency compares useful hydraulic power with supplied input power.

<EquationBlock

## Hydraulic Power

# Best Efficiency Point (BEP)

<span className="highlight">Best Efficiency Point (BEP)</span> is the most stable operating condition of a pump.

Operating away from BEP may cause:

- Vibration
- Seal failure
- Bearing damage
- Cavitation
- Power wastage

---

# NPSH

<span className="highlight">Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH)</span> is a critical pump operating parameter.

## NPSHa

Available suction energy from the system.

---

## NPSHr

Minimum suction energy required by the pump.

Required condition:

<EquationBlock
  title="NPSH Condition"
  equation="NPSHa > NPSHr"
  description="Available suction head must remain greater than required suction head to prevent cavitation."
/>

<WarningBox>
If NPSHa becomes lower than NPSHr, cavitation may occur inside the pump.
</WarningBox>

---

# Cavitation

<span className="highlight">Cavitation</span> is one of the most critical pump operational problems.

Cause:

- Local pressure drops below vapor pressure

Effects include:

- Noise
- Vibration
- Pitting
- Impeller damage
- Flow reduction
- Seal failure

Prevention methods include:

- Increase suction head
- Reduce temperature
- Use larger suction pipe
- Lower flow rate
- Reduce friction losses

<WarningBox>
Severe cavitation can rapidly damage impellers and reduce pump life.
</WarningBox>

# Practical Industrial Understanding

## Why suction pipe is larger

Suction pipes are kept larger to reduce friction losses and avoid cavitation at the pump inlet.

---

## Why discharge valve remains closed during startup

The discharge valve is initially kept closed to reduce starting load and prevent sudden flow surge.

---

## Why minimum flow line is needed

Minimum flow lines prevent overheating and internal damage when the pump operates at very low flow conditions.

---

## Why cavitation damages impellers

Cavitation creates vapor bubbles that collapse violently and cause pitting and erosion on impeller surfaces.

---

## Why dead-heading is dangerous

Dead-heading stops fluid movement, causing overheating, pressure buildup, and possible pump damage.

---

## Why recirculation occurs

Recirculation occurs when flow becomes unstable inside the pump due to operation away from design conditions.
