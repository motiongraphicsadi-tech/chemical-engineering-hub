# Fick's Laws of Diffusion Introduction

Diffusion is one of the most fundamental mechanisms of mass transfer. To quantitatively describe diffusion, Adolf Fick proposed a set of mathematical relationships known as Fick's Laws of Diffusion.

These laws relate diffusion flux to concentration gradients and explain how concentration changes with time and position.

Fick's laws form the foundation of:

- Mass transfer theory
- Separation process design
- Membrane systems
- Gas absorption
- Distillation
- Drying operations
- Catalytic reactor analysis

Nearly every mass transfer calculation in chemical engineering begins with Fick's laws.

---

# Core Concept

Diffusion occurs because molecules move randomly and tend to migrate from regions of higher concentration to lower concentration.

The driving force is:

$$
\text{Concentration Gradient}
$$

Fick's laws quantify this movement.

### Fick's First Law

Describes steady-state diffusion.

### Fick's Second Law

Describes unsteady-state diffusion.

Together they form the mathematical basis of molecular mass transfer.

---

# Classification and Types

## Fick's First Law

Used for:

- Steady-state diffusion
- Constant concentration profile with time

Applications:

- Gas diffusion through films
- Membrane transport
- Steady absorption systems

---

## Fick's Second Law

Used for:

- Unsteady-state diffusion
- Time-dependent concentration changes

Applications:

- Drug release systems
- Drying operations
- Diffusion into solids

---

# Working Principle

The diffusion process follows these steps:

1. A concentration difference exists.
2. Molecules move randomly.
3. Net movement occurs toward lower concentration.
4. Diffusion flux develops.
5. Concentration profiles evolve.
6. Equilibrium is eventually reached.

At equilibrium:

$$
\frac{dC_A}{dz}=0
$$

and diffusion ceases.

---

# Mathematical Definition

## Fick's First Law

The diffusion flux is proportional to the negative concentration gradient.

$$
N_A
=
-D_{AB}
\frac{dC_A}{dz}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Description | Unit |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| $N_A$ | Diffusion Flux | mol/m²·s |
| $D_{AB}$ | Diffusivity | m²/s |
| $C_A$ | Concentration of A | mol/m³ |
| $z$ | Direction of Diffusion | m |

---

## Fick's Second Law

Describes concentration variation with time.

$$
\frac{\partial C_A}{\partial t}
=
D_{AB}
\frac{\partial^2 C_A}{\partial z^2}
$$

This equation governs transient diffusion phenomena.

---

# Assumptions

For deriving Fick's laws:

- Isothermal system
- Constant diffusivity
- Homogeneous medium
- No chemical reaction
- One-dimensional diffusion
- Continuum behavior

These assumptions simplify analysis.

# Derivation and Theory

## Derivation of Fick's First Law

Experimental observations show:

$$
N_A
\propto
-\frac{dC_A}{dz}
$$

Introducing the proportionality constant \(D_{AB}\):

$$
N_A
=
-D_{AB}
\frac{dC_A}{dz}
$$

This is Fick's First Law.

---

## Derivation of Fick's Second Law

Consider a differential volume element.

Mass balance:

$$
\text{Accumulation}
=
\text{In}
-
\text{Out}
$$

Combining mass conservation with Fick's First Law gives:

$$
\frac{\partial C_A}{\partial t}
=
D_{AB}
\frac{\partial^2 C_A}{\partial z^2}
$$

This equation predicts concentration changes with time.

# Key Relationships and Equations

## Diffusion Flux

$$
N_A

-D_{AB}

$$

---

## Fick's Second Law

$$
\frac{\partial C_A}{\partial t}
=
D_{AB}
\frac{\partial^2 C_A}{\partial z^2}
$$

---

## Equimolar Counter Diffusion

$$
N_A=-N_B
$$

---

## Flux Through Plane Film

$$
N_A
=
D_{AB}
\frac{C_{A1}-C_{A2}}{L}
$$

Where:

- \(L\) = Diffusion Path Length

---

## Total Diffusion Rate

$$
\dot n_A
=
N_AA
$$

Where:

- \(A\) = Area

# Solved Example

### Problem

A gas diffuses through a stagnant film.

Given:

$$
D_{AB}
=
2.5\times10^{-5}
\;m^2/s
$$

Film thickness:

$$
L=0.01\,m
$$

Concentration difference:

$$
C_{A1}-C_{A2}
=
100
\;mol/m^3
$$

Calculate diffusion flux.

### Given

- \(D_{AB}=2.5\times10^{-5}\;m^2/s\)
- \(L=0.01\,m\)
- \(\Delta C_A=100\,mol/m^3\)

### Solution

Using:

$$
N_A
=
D_{AB}
\frac{C_{A1}-C_{A2}}{L}
$$

Substituting:

$$
N_A
=
\frac{(2.5\times10^{-5})(100)}
{0.01}
$$

$$
N_A
=
0.25
\;mol/m^2.s
$$

### Final Answer

$$
N_A
=
0.25
\;mol/m^2.s
$$

# Summary

Fick's Laws of Diffusion provide the mathematical foundation for molecular mass transfer. Fick's First Law relates diffusion flux to concentration gradient under steady-state conditions, while Fick's Second Law describes concentration changes with time during transient diffusion.

These laws are extensively used in the design and analysis of absorbers, distillation columns, membrane systems, drying equipment, catalytic reactors, and numerous other chemical engineering operations. Mastery of Fick's laws is essential for understanding advanced mass transfer phenomena and industrial separation processes.
