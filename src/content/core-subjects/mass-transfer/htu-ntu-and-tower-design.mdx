# HTU, NTU and Tower Design Introduction

Packed towers are widely used in absorption, stripping, distillation, humidification, and other gas-liquid mass transfer operations.

A major design challenge is determining:

- Tower height
- Tower diameter
- Packing requirements
- Separation performance

To simplify tower design, engineers use the concepts of:

- Height of Transfer Unit (HTU)
- Number of Transfer Units (NTU)

These concepts provide a practical method for estimating the height required to achieve a desired separation.

HTU-NTU analysis is one of the most important tools in packed tower design and is extensively used in industrial mass transfer calculations.

---

# Why It Matters

HTU and NTU concepts help engineers:

- Design packed towers
- Estimate tower height
- Evaluate packing performance
- Compare packing materials
- Scale laboratory data to industrial units

Applications include:

- Gas absorption
- Stripping
- Humidification
- Cooling towers
- Environmental control systems

Almost every industrial packed tower design involves HTU and NTU calculations.

---

# Core Concept

Packed tower design is based on the relationship:

$$
Z
=
HTU \times NTU
$$

Where:

- \(Z\) = Tower height
- \(HTU\) = Height of Transfer Unit
- \(NTU\) = Number of Transfer Units

The concept separates:

### Mass Transfer Efficiency

Represented by:

$$
HTU
$$

---

### Separation Requirement

Represented by:

$$
NTU
$$

This separation greatly simplifies tower design calculations.

---

# Classification and Types

Transfer units can be expressed on different bases.

## Gas-Phase Transfer Units

Used when gas-phase resistance dominates.

Examples:

$$

$$

## Liquid-Phase Transfer Units

Used when liquid-phase resistance dominates.

Examples:

$$
HTU_L
$$

$$
NTU_L
$$

---

## Overall Transfer Units

Most commonly used in industrial design.

Examples:

$$
HTU_{OG}

$$

# Working Principle

The HTU-NTU method works as follows:

1. Determine equilibrium relationship.
2. Construct operating line.
3. Evaluate driving force.
4. Calculate NTU.
5. Obtain HTU from correlations or experiments.
6. Calculate tower height.

Finally:

$$
Z
=
HTU \times NTU
$$

The result gives the required packed height.

---

# Mathematical Definition

# Height of Transfer Unit (HTU)

HTU represents the height required for one transfer unit.

Definition:

$$
HTU
=
\frac{G}{K_Ga}
$$

Gas-phase basis.

Where:

- \(G\) = Gas mass velocity
- \(K_G\) = Overall mass transfer coefficient
- \(a\) = Interfacial area

Units:

$$
m
$$

---

# Number of Transfer Units (NTU)

NTU measures separation difficulty.

Definition:

$$
NTU
=
\int
\frac{dy}
{y-y^*}
$$

Where:

- \(y\) = Actual composition
- \(y^*\) = Equilibrium composition

NTU is dimensionless.

---

# Packed Tower Height

Combining HTU and NTU:

$$
Z
=
HTU \times NTU
$$

This is the fundamental packed tower design equation.

---

# Assumptions

Common assumptions include:

- Steady-state operation
- Constant flow rates
- Constant physical properties
- Negligible heat effects
- Known equilibrium relationship
- Uniform packing characteristics

These assumptions simplify design calculations.

---

# Derivation and Theory

# Differential Tower Section

Consider a small tower element:

$$
dZ
$$

Mass transfer occurs through this differential height.

The transfer rate is proportional to:

$$
(y-y^*)
$$

which represents the driving force.

---

# Tower Height Expression

Integrating across the tower gives:

$$
Z
=
HTU
\int
\frac{dy}
{y-y^*}
$$

The integral term becomes NTU.

Therefore:

$$
Z
=
HTU \times NTU
$$

This equation forms the foundation of packed tower design.

---

# Physical Meaning of HTU

Small HTU:

- Efficient packing
- High mass transfer coefficient
- Shorter tower

Large HTU:

- Poor mass transfer
- Taller tower required

---

# Physical Meaning of NTU

Small NTU:

- Easy separation

Large NTU:

- Difficult separation
- Greater tower height required

# Solved Example

### Problem

A packed absorption tower requires:

$$
NTU=8
$$

The packing provides:

$$
HTU=0.75 \; m
$$

Calculate required tower height.

### Given

- \(NTU=8\)
- \(HTU=0.75\;m\)

### Solution

Using:

$$
Z
=
HTU \times NTU
$$

Substituting:

$$
Z
=
0.75 \times 8
$$

$$
Z
=
6.0\;m
$$

### Final Answer

$$
Z
=
6.0\;m
$$

Required packed height is:

$$
6.0\;m
$$

- Ignoring flooding limitations

- Mixing gas-phase and liquid-phase bases

# Summary

The HTU-NTU method is one of the most important design approaches used for packed towers in mass transfer operations. HTU represents the efficiency of mass transfer while NTU represents the separation difficulty.

By combining these concepts through the equation \(Z = HTU \times NTU\), engineers can determine the required tower height for absorption, stripping, humidification, and other gas-liquid operations. The HTU-NTU approach provides a practical and widely accepted framework for industrial packed tower design.
