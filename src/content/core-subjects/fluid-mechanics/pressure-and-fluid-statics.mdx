# Introduction

Fluid Statics is the branch of fluid mechanics that deals with fluids at rest.

When a fluid is stationary:

- No velocity gradients exist.
- No shear stresses act.
- Only normal pressure forces are present.

The study of pressure distribution in static fluids is essential for analyzing:

- Storage tanks
- Pressure vessels
- Hydraulic systems
- Manometers
- Dams

Fluid statics forms the foundation for understanding pressure measurement and hydrostatic forces.

---

# Why It Matters

Chemical industries store large quantities of:

- Water
- Chemicals
- Petroleum products
- Process fluids

Engineers must determine:

- Pressure at different depths
- Forces acting on tank walls
- Liquid level measurements
- Hydraulic system performance

Fluid statics helps engineers safely design and operate equipment subjected to fluid pressure.

---

# Definition

Fluid statics is the study of fluids that remain at rest.

Characteristics:

- Fluid velocity equals zero.
- Shear stress equals zero.
- Pressure acts equally in all directions.

The primary concern in fluid statics is pressure distribution.

---

# Physical Significance

Pressure is one of the most important properties in fluid mechanics.

Pressure determines:

- Force on surfaces
- Fluid storage behavior
- Hydraulic system operation
- Fluid measurement techniques

In static fluids, pressure increases with depth because of the weight of the fluid above.

This principle governs many engineering applications.

---

# Mathematical Definition

Pressure is defined as force acting per unit area.

$$
P
=
\frac{F}{A}
$$

Where:

- \(P\) = Pressure
- \(F\) = Force
- \(A\) = Area

SI Unit:

$$
Pa
=
N/m^2
$$

Pressure is a scalar quantity and acts normal to surfaces.

---

# Fluid Statics

A fluid at rest cannot sustain shear stress.

Therefore:

$$
\tau = 0
$$

Only normal stresses exist.

These normal stresses are referred to as pressure.

In static fluids:

- Pressure acts equally in all directions.
- Pressure increases with depth.
- Pressure depends only on fluid density and depth.

These principles form the basis of fluid statics.

---

# Pressure Concept

Pressure is generated whenever a force acts on a fluid.

Mathematically:

$$
P
=
\frac{F}{A}
$$

Pressure can be viewed as:

- Energy per unit volume
- Force per unit area

Higher pressure means a larger force acting on a given area.

---

# Absolute Pressure

Absolute pressure is measured relative to a perfect vacuum.

Relationship:

$$
P_{abs}
=
P_{atm}
+
P_{gauge}
$$

Where:

- \(P_{abs}\) = Absolute Pressure
- \(P_{atm}\) = Atmospheric Pressure
- \(P_{gauge}\) = Gauge Pressure

Absolute pressure can never be negative.

---

# Gauge Pressure

Gauge pressure is measured relative to atmospheric pressure.

Mathematically:

$$
P_{gauge}
=
P_{abs}
-
P_{atm}
$$

Examples:

- Tire pressure
- Boiler pressure
- Compressed air systems

Most industrial pressure gauges measure gauge pressure.

---

# Vacuum Pressure

Vacuum pressure occurs when fluid pressure is lower than atmospheric pressure.

Mathematically:

$$
P_{vac}
=
P_{atm}
-
P_{abs}
$$

Examples:

- Vacuum distillation
- Vacuum drying
- Vacuum pumps

Vacuum systems are widely used in chemical industries.

---

# Pressure Head

Pressure head represents pressure expressed as an equivalent height of fluid.

Mathematically:

$$
h
=
\frac{P}{\rho g}
$$

Where:

- \(h\) = Pressure Head
- \(P\) = Pressure
- \(\rho\) = Density
- \(g\) = Gravitational Acceleration

Unit:

$$
m
$$

Pressure head is extensively used in hydraulic calculations.

---

# Pascal's Law

Pascal's Law states:

> Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.

Mathematically:

$$
P_1=P_2
$$

Applications:

- Hydraulic Presses
- Hydraulic Lifts
- Hydraulic Brakes

Pascal's Law forms the basis of hydraulic systems.

---

# Hydrostatic Law

Hydrostatic law describes pressure variation with depth.

Mathematically:

$$
P
=
P_0
+
\rho gh
$$

Where:

- \(P\) = Pressure at Depth
- \(P_0\) = Surface Pressure
- \(\rho\) = Fluid Density
- \(g\) = Gravitational Acceleration
- \(h\) = Depth

This equation shows that pressure increases linearly with depth.

---

# Factors Affecting It

Several factors influence fluid pressure.

### Depth

Pressure increases as depth increases.

### Density

Higher density fluids generate greater pressure.

### Gravity

Pressure depends directly on gravitational acceleration.

### Atmospheric Pressure

Affects absolute pressure calculations.

### Fluid Type

Different fluids produce different hydrostatic pressures.

# Common Mistakes

Students often:

- Confuse gauge and absolute pressure
- Ignore atmospheric pressure
- Use incorrect pressure units
- Forget pressure varies with depth
- Misapply Pascal's Law

<WarningBox>
Gauge pressure is measured relative to atmospheric pressure, whereas absolute pressure is measured relative to a perfect vacuum.
</WarningBox>

# Key Takeaways

- Fluid statics deals with fluids at rest.
- Pressure is force per unit area.
- Static fluids cannot sustain shear stress.
- Absolute pressure is measured from vacuum.
- Gauge pressure is measured from atmospheric pressure.
- Vacuum pressure occurs below atmospheric pressure.
- Pressure head converts pressure into fluid height.
- Pascal's Law governs hydraulic systems.
- Hydrostatic pressure increases with depth.
