# Wien's Displacement Law

<TopicRoadmap />


## Why do furnace tubes glow red while steam pipes do not?

A steam pipe operating at 250°C is hot enough to burn your hand.

Yet it does not glow.

A furnace tube operating at much higher temperatures often glows red.

Why?

The answer lies in **Wien's Displacement Law**, which relates temperature to the wavelength at which radiation is emitted most strongly.

---

# What Is Wien's Displacement Law?

Wien's Displacement Law predicts the wavelength corresponding to the maximum radiation emitted by a hot body.



Where:

| Symbol | Description |
|----------|----------|
| \(\lambda_{max}\) | Peak wavelength (μm) |
| \(T\) | Absolute temperature (K) |

---

# What Does the Law Tell Us?

The law shows that:

- As temperature increases, peak wavelength decreases.
- Hotter objects emit radiation at shorter wavelengths.
- Cooler objects emit radiation at longer wavelengths.

Therefore:

$$
\lambda_{max} \propto \frac{1}{T}
$$

---

# Examples

## Human Body

Temperature:

$$
T \approx 310 K
$$

Peak wavelength:

$$
\lambda_{max} \approx 9.3 \, \mu m
$$

This lies in the infrared region.

Therefore, humans do not glow visibly.

---

## Steam Pipe

Temperature:

$$
T \approx 523 K
$$

Peak wavelength:

$$
\lambda_{max} \approx 5.5 \, \mu m
$$

Still in the infrared region.

Therefore, steam pipes emit invisible thermal radiation.

---

## Furnace Tube

Temperature:

$$
T \approx 1073 K
$$

Peak wavelength:

$$
\lambda_{max} \approx 2.7 \, \mu m
$$

The radiation shifts toward shorter wavelengths, and part of the emission becomes visible as a dull red glow.

---

## Sun

Temperature:

$$
T \approx 5778 K
$$

Peak wavelength:

$$
\lambda_{max} \approx 0.5 \, \mu m
$$

This lies in the visible region.

That is why sunlight is visible to our eyes.

---

# Visualization

| Object | Temperature (K) | Peak Wavelength (μm) |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Human Body | 310 | 9.3 |

| Furnace Tube | 1073 | 2.7 |
| Sun | 5778 | 0.5 |

Notice:

- Temperature ↑
- Peak wavelength ↓

# Engineering Significance

Wien's Law helps engineers:

- Understand thermal radiation behavior
- Predict dominant radiation wavelengths
- Design infrared sensors
- Select thermal imaging equipment
- Analyze furnace and boiler performance

It also explains why infrared cameras can detect equipment temperatures without direct contact.

---

# Industrial Insight

When a process engineer sees:

- A steam pipe → Think infrared radiation.
- A hot reactor → Think shorter infrared wavelengths.
- A glowing furnace tube → Radiation entering the visible region.

The hotter the object, the shorter the wavelength at which it emits most strongly.

# Key Takeaways

- Wien's Displacement Law relates temperature and peak wavelength.
- The law is given by:

$$
\lambda_{max} = \frac{2898}{T}
$$

- Hotter objects emit radiation at shorter wavelengths.
- Human bodies emit mainly infrared radiation.
- Steam pipes emit infrared radiation.
- Furnace tubes emit shorter wavelength radiation and may glow red.
- The Sun emits strongly in the visible region.

---

# Summary

Wien's Displacement Law states that the wavelength of maximum radiation emission is inversely proportional to temperature. As temperature increases, the peak wavelength shifts toward shorter values. This explains why steam pipes emit invisible infrared radiation, furnace tubes glow red, and the Sun emits visible light. Wien's Law is one of the most important tools for understanding thermal radiation and infrared technology.
