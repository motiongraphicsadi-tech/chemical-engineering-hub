# Heat Transfer Coefficient & Boundary Layer

---

# Introduction

Heat transfer between a surface and fluid mainly occurs through convection.

The effectiveness of convection depends on:
- boundary layer formation
- fluid properties
- flow pattern
- turbulence
- velocity

Understanding these concepts is important for:
- heat exchanger design
- cooling systems
- boiler operation
- reactor cooling
- industrial heat transfer calculations

<IndustrialInsight>
  Heat transfer coefficient and boundary layer behavior directly affect industrial equipment efficiency and energy consumption.
</IndustrialInsight>

---

## 7. What is boundary layer?

<InterviewQuestion
  question="What is boundary layer?"
  answer="Boundary layer is the thin region near a surface where velocity or temperature changes rapidly."
/>

# Boundary Layer Concept

When fluid flows over a surface:
- fluid velocity becomes zero at the wall
- velocity gradually increases away from the wall
- temperature also changes near the surface

This thin region is called the boundary layer.

## Importance of Boundary Layer

Boundary layer affects:
- heat transfer rate
- frictional losses
- pressure drop
- fluid flow behavior

<IndustrialInsight>
  Boundary layer thickness strongly influences heat exchanger performance and fluid flow efficiency in industrial systems.
</IndustrialInsight>

# 8. Types of Boundary Layer

## 1. Hydrodynamic Boundary Layer

<InterviewQuestion
  question="What is hydrodynamic boundary layer?"
  answer="Hydrodynamic boundary layer is the region where fluid velocity changes from zero at the wall to free stream velocity."
/>

## Characteristics

- related to velocity distribution
- formed due to fluid viscosity
- increases along flow direction

---

# 2. Thermal Boundary Layer

<InterviewQuestion
  question="What is thermal boundary layer?"
  answer="Thermal boundary layer is the region where temperature changes from surface temperature to fluid temperature."
/>

## Characteristics

- related to temperature distribution
- affects heat transfer rate
- depends on thermal conductivity

<WarningBox>
  Thermal and hydrodynamic boundary layer thickness may not always be equal because momentum transfer and heat transfer occur differently.
</WarningBox>

## 8.1 What is Prandtl number?

<InterviewQuestion
  question="What is Prandtl number?"
  answer="Prandtl number is the ratio of momentum diffusivity to thermal diffusivity."
/>

<EquationBlock
  title="Prandtl Number"
  equation="Pr = μCp/k"
  description="Prandtl number compares momentum diffusivity with thermal diffusivity."
/>

Where:
- μ = viscosity
- Cp = specific heat
- k = thermal conductivity

## Importance of Prandtl Number

Prandtl number helps determine:
- relative thickness of boundary layers
- heat transfer behavior
- convection characteristics

## Interpretation

- high Pr → slower heat diffusion
- low Pr → faster heat diffusion

Heat transfer coefficient is represented by:

- h

- effectiveness of convection

## 11. Convective heat transfer correlation or Newton’s law of cooling?

<InterviewQuestion
  question="What is Newton’s law of cooling?"
  answer="Newton’s law of cooling states that heat transfer rate is proportional to temperature difference between surface and fluid."
/>

<EquationBlock
  title="Newton's Law of Cooling"
  equation="Q = hA(Ts - Tf)"
  description="Convective heat transfer rate depends on heat transfer coefficient, area, and temperature difference."
/>

Where:
- Q = heat transfer rate
- h = heat transfer coefficient
- A = surface area
- Ts = surface temperature
- Tf = fluid temperature

## Importance

Newton’s law of cooling is widely used in:
- heat exchanger calculations
- cooling systems
- industrial heat transfer analysis

## 12. Difference between natural vs forced convection?

<InterviewQuestion
  question="What is the difference between natural and forced convection?"
  answer="Natural convection occurs due to density difference, while forced convection occurs due to external force such as pump or fan."
/>

| Natural Convection | Forced Convection |
|---|---|
| Caused by density difference | Caused by external force |
| Slower heat transfer | Faster heat transfer |
| No pump or fan required | Pump or fan required |
| Lower heat transfer coefficient | Higher heat transfer coefficient |

## Examples

### Natural Convection
- room cooling
- hot air rising

### Forced Convection
- heat exchangers
- cooling fans
- pumped cooling systems

## 13. What increases convection?

<InterviewQuestion
  question="What increases convection heat transfer?"
  answer="Convection increases with higher fluid velocity, turbulence, larger temperature difference, and higher surface area."
/>

# Factors Increasing Convection

- higher fluid velocity
- turbulence
- increased surface area
- larger temperature difference
- better fluid mixing

<IndustrialInsight>
  Industrial heat exchangers often use turbulence promoters and higher fluid velocity to improve heat transfer performance.
</IndustrialInsight>

---

## 14. Why turbulent flow gives higher heat transfer?

<InterviewQuestion
  question="Why does turbulent flow give higher heat transfer?"
  answer="Turbulent flow gives higher heat transfer because it improves fluid mixing and reduces thermal boundary layer thickness."
/>

# Effect of Turbulence

Turbulent flow:
- improves mixing
- reduces boundary layer thickness
- increases temperature uniformity
- increases heat transfer coefficient

## Industrial Importance

Turbulent flow is preferred in:
- heat exchangers
- cooling systems
- reactor jackets
- industrial piping systems

<WarningBox>
  Although turbulent flow improves heat transfer, it also increases pressure drop and pumping power requirement.
</WarningBox>
