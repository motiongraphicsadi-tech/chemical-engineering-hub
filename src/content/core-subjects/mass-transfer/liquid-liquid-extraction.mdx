# Liquid-Liquid Extraction Introduction

Liquid-liquid extraction (LLE), also known as solvent extraction, is a separation process in which a solute is transferred from one liquid phase to another liquid phase that is partially or completely immiscible.

Unlike distillation, which relies on volatility differences, extraction utilizes differences in solubility between two liquid phases.

Liquid-liquid extraction is widely used when:

- Components have similar boiling points
- Distillation is energy-intensive
- Heat-sensitive materials are involved
- High-purity separations are required

The process plays an important role in chemical, pharmaceutical, food, petroleum, and metallurgical industries.

---

# Why It Matters

Liquid-liquid extraction is important because it allows:

- Separation of difficult mixtures
- Recovery of valuable products
- Purification of chemicals
- Processing of heat-sensitive compounds
- Reduction of energy consumption

Industrial applications include:

- Pharmaceutical purification
- Aromatics extraction
- Metal recovery
- Vegetable oil processing
- Nuclear fuel reprocessing

It is one of the most important equilibrium-based separation operations in chemical engineering.

---

# Core Concept

Liquid-liquid extraction involves three major components:

- Feed
- Solvent
- Solute

The solvent selectively dissolves the desired solute from the feed phase.

After mixing and phase separation:

- Extract phase → Solvent-rich phase containing solute
- Raffinate phase → Feed-rich phase depleted of solute

The extent of separation depends on equilibrium distribution of the solute between the two phases.

---

# Classification and Types

## Single-Stage Extraction

Only one contact between feed and solvent.

Simple operation.

Used when:

- High distribution coefficient exists
- Separation requirements are moderate

---

## Multistage Extraction

Multiple extraction stages are used.

Advantages:

- Higher recovery
- Better separation

Most industrial extraction systems use multistage operation.

---

## Crosscurrent Extraction

Fresh solvent enters each stage.

Simple design.

---

## Countercurrent Extraction

Feed and solvent flow in opposite directions.

Highest extraction efficiency.

Most widely used industrial arrangement.

---

# Working Principle

The extraction process occurs through the following steps:

1. Feed enters extractor.
2. Solvent is added.
3. Mixing creates intimate contact.
4. Solute transfers between phases.
5. Equilibrium is established.
6. Phases separate due to density differences.
7. Extract and raffinate streams leave the system.

Repeated contacting improves recovery and separation.

---

# Mathematical Definition

The equilibrium distribution of solute is often represented by:

$$
K_D
=
\frac{y}{x}
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| $K_D$ | Distribution Coefficient |
| $y$ | Solute Concentration in Extract Phase |
| $x$ | Solute Concentration in Raffinate Phase |

The distribution coefficient indicates how strongly a solute prefers the solvent phase.

---

# Assumptions

Common assumptions include:

- Equilibrium achieved between phases
- Constant temperature
- Negligible solvent losses
- Immiscible liquid phases
- Uniform mixing
- No chemical reaction

These assumptions simplify extraction calculations.

---

# Derivation and Theory

# Distribution Law

According to Nernst Distribution Law:

$$
K_D
=
\frac{C_E}{C_R}
$$

Where:

- \(C_E\) = Solute concentration in extract
- \(C_R\) = Solute concentration in raffinate

At constant temperature, this ratio remains approximately constant.

---

# Extraction Equilibrium

Equilibrium relationships determine:

- Solute recovery
- Solvent requirements
- Number of stages

The equilibrium curve is obtained experimentally.

---

# Solvent Selection Theory

An ideal solvent should:

- Have high selectivity
- Have high distribution coefficient
- Be immiscible with feed
- Be chemically stable
- Be inexpensive
- Be easy to recover

Solvent selection is one of the most important design decisions.

# Solved Example

### Problem

A solute has:

$$
K_D=4
$$

The concentration in the raffinate phase is:

$$
x=0.05
$$

Calculate the equilibrium concentration in the extract phase.

### Given

- \(K_D=4\)
- \(x=0.05\)

### Solution

Using:

$$
K_D
=
\frac{y}{x}
$$

Rearranging:

$$
y
=
K_Dx
$$

Substituting:

$$
y
=
4(0.05)
$$

$$
y
=
0.20
$$

### Final Answer

$$
y=0.20
$$

The equilibrium extract concentration is 0.20.

# Summary

Liquid-liquid extraction is a powerful separation process that uses differences in solubility to transfer a solute between two immiscible liquid phases. The process depends on equilibrium distribution, solvent selection, and mass transfer principles.

Key concepts include distribution coefficient, extract and raffinate streams, single-stage and multistage extraction, and solvent selection criteria. Liquid-liquid extraction is widely used in chemical, pharmaceutical, petroleum, food, and metallurgical industries for efficient and selective separations.
