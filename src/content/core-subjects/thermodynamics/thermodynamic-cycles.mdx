# Introduction

Thermodynamic cycles are sequences of thermodynamic processes that return a system to its initial state.

These cycles form the basis of:

- Power generation systems
- Internal combustion engines
- Refrigeration systems
- Air conditioning systems
- Heat pumps

In a cycle:

$$
Initial\ State = Final\ State
$$

Therefore:

$$
\Delta U = 0
$$

over one complete cycle.

---

# Why It Matters

Most industrial energy systems operate on thermodynamic cycles.

Examples include:

- Steam power plants
- Gas turbines
- Automobiles
- Refrigeration plants
- Aircraft engines

Understanding thermodynamic cycles helps engineers:

- Improve efficiency
- Reduce fuel consumption
- Optimize energy usage
- Design thermal systems

---

# Core Concept

A thermodynamic cycle consists of a series of processes through which a working fluid passes.

At the end of the cycle:

$$
\Delta E_{cycle}=0
$$

However:

$$
Q \neq 0
$$

and

$$
W \neq 0
$$

meaning heat and work interactions occur during the cycle.

---

# Working Principle

Most thermodynamic cycles operate using:

1. Heat Addition
2. Expansion
3. Heat Rejection
4. Compression

The cycle then repeats continuously.

For power cycles:

$$
W_{out}>W_{in}
$$

For refrigeration cycles:

$$
W_{in}>0
$$

to move heat from a colder region to a hotter region.

---

# Mathematical Definition

For a cyclic process:

$$
\Delta U = 0
$$

Applying the First Law:

$$
Q_{net}=W_{net}
$$

Thermal efficiency:

$$
\eta=\frac{W_{net}}{Q_{in}}
$$

This is the fundamental efficiency equation for power cycles.

---

# Power Cycles

Power cycles convert thermal energy into mechanical work.

Major power cycles include:

- Rankine Cycle
- Reheat Rankine Cycle
- Regenerative Rankine Cycle
- Brayton Cycle
- Otto Cycle
- Diesel Cycle
- Dual Cycle

---

# Rankine Cycle

The Rankine Cycle is the most important steam power cycle.

Main components:

- Boiler
- Turbine
- Condenser
- Pump

Processes:

1. Pumping
2. Heat Addition
3. Expansion
4. Condensation

Applications:

- Thermal Power Plants
- Nuclear Power Plants

---

# Reheat Rankine Cycle

A modification of the Rankine cycle.

Steam expands partially in a turbine.

It is then reheated before entering another turbine stage.

Benefits:

- Higher efficiency
- Reduced turbine blade moisture
- Improved power output

Widely used in modern power plants.

---

# Regenerative Rankine Cycle

Part of the steam is extracted from the turbine and used to preheat feedwater.

Benefits:

- Improved thermal efficiency
- Reduced fuel consumption
- Better cycle performance

Common in large utility power stations.

---

# Brayton Cycle

The Brayton Cycle is the ideal cycle for gas turbines.

Main processes:

1. Compression
2. Heat Addition
3. Expansion
4. Heat Rejection

Applications:

- Gas Turbine Power Plants
- Aircraft Engines

---

# Otto Cycle

The Otto Cycle is the ideal cycle for spark ignition engines.

Examples:

- Petrol Engines
- Gasoline Engines

Characteristics:

- Constant volume heat addition
- High-speed operation

Applications:

- Cars
- Motorcycles

---

# Diesel Cycle

The Diesel Cycle is the ideal cycle for compression ignition engines.

Characteristics:

- Constant pressure heat addition
- Higher compression ratio

Applications:

- Trucks
- Ships
- Diesel Generators

---

# Dual Cycle

The Dual Cycle combines features of:

- Otto Cycle
- Diesel Cycle

Heat addition occurs partly at:

- Constant Volume
- Constant Pressure

It better represents real internal combustion engines.

---

# Refrigeration Cycles

Refrigeration cycles transfer heat from low temperature regions to high temperature regions.

Work input is required.

Major refrigeration cycles include:

- Vapor Compression Cycle
- Vapor Absorption Cycle
- Air Refrigeration Cycle

---

# Vapor Compression Cycle

Most common refrigeration cycle.

Main components:

- Compressor
- Condenser
- Expansion Valve
- Evaporator

Applications:

- Domestic Refrigerators
- Air Conditioners
- Cold Storage Plants

---

# Vapor Absorption Cycle

Uses heat instead of mechanical compression.

Common absorbent pairs:

- Ammonia-Water
- Lithium Bromide-Water

Advantages:

- Low electricity consumption
- Suitable for waste heat utilization

---

# Air Refrigeration Cycle

Uses air as the refrigerant.

Applications:

- Aircraft Cooling Systems
- Cryogenic Systems

Advantages:

- Simple operation
- Environmentally friendly

---

# P-V Diagrams

Pressure-Volume diagrams represent cycle behavior graphically.

Axes:

- Pressure (P)
- Volume (V)

Applications:

- Work calculations
- Cycle analysis
- Engine studies

Important cycles commonly represented on P-V diagrams:

- Otto Cycle
- Diesel Cycle
- Dual Cycle

Area enclosed by the cycle:

$$
Area = Net\ Work
$$

---

# T-S Diagrams

Temperature-Entropy diagrams are widely used in thermodynamics.

Axes:

- Temperature (T)
- Entropy (S)

Applications:

- Heat transfer analysis
- Power cycle analysis
- Refrigeration cycle analysis

For reversible processes:

$$
Area = Heat\ Transfer
$$

T-S diagrams are extensively used in Rankine Cycle studies.

---

# Efficiency Calculations

Thermal efficiency of power cycles:

$$
\eta
=
\frac{W_{net}}{Q_{in}}
$$

Where:

- $W_{net}$ = Net Work Output
- $Q_{in}$ = Heat Input

Higher efficiency means better energy utilization.

# Limitations

Ideal cycles:

- Ignore friction
- Ignore heat losses
- Ignore pressure drops

Real systems always perform below ideal cycle predictions.

Cycle analysis must be combined with practical considerations.

# Solved Example

A power cycle receives:

$$
Q_{in}=1200\;kJ
$$

and produces:

$$
W_{net}=480\;kJ
$$

Efficiency:

$$
\eta
=
\frac{W_{net}}{Q_{in}}
$$

Substituting:

$$
\eta
=
\frac{480}{1200}
$$

$$
\eta
=
0.40
$$

Therefore:

$$
\eta = 40\%
$$

---

# Common Mistakes

Students commonly:

- Confuse power cycles with refrigeration cycles
- Forget cyclic processes have zero internal energy change
- Use incorrect efficiency equations
- Ignore work input requirements in refrigeration systems
- Misinterpret P-V and T-S diagrams

<WarningBox>
For a complete cycle, the system returns to its initial state, therefore ΔU = 0 over the entire cycle.
</WarningBox>

# Key Takeaways

- Thermodynamic cycles return to their initial state.
- Power cycles convert heat into work.
- Refrigeration cycles require work input.
- Rankine Cycle is used in steam power plants.
- Brayton Cycle is used in gas turbines.
- Otto and Diesel cycles model internal combustion engines.
- Vapor Compression Cycle is the most common refrigeration cycle.
- P-V and T-S diagrams are essential analysis tools.
