# Black Body Radiation Curve

<TopicRoadmap />


## Is Radiation Emitted at Only One Wavelength?

A common misconception is that a hot object emits radiation at only one wavelength.

This is not true.

A hot surface emits radiation over a **range of wavelengths**.

Some wavelengths are emitted more strongly than others, creating a characteristic radiation distribution known as the **Black Body Radiation Curve**.

---

# Radiation Distribution

Every black body emits radiation across a continuous spectrum.

```text
Low Intensity                         High Intensity

        /\ 
       /  \
      /    \
     /      \
____/        \________________

← Short λ                Long λ →
```

The curve shows:

- Radiation intensity on the vertical axis.
- Wavelength on the horizontal axis.

---

# Black Body Radiation Curve

The shape of the curve represents how radiation is distributed across wavelengths.

- Radiation is emitted over many wavelengths.
- One wavelength has the maximum intensity.
- The wavelength corresponding to the peak is called the **peak wavelength**.

---

# Effect of Temperature

As temperature increases:

1. Total radiation emitted increases.
2. Peak radiation intensity increases.
3. Peak wavelength shifts toward shorter wavelengths.

This behavior explains why hotter objects emit more energy and appear brighter.

# What the Curve Tells Us

### Low Temperature Object

- Lower radiation intensity
- Longer peak wavelength

Examples:

- Human body
- Warm water tank
- Steam pipe

---

### High Temperature Object

- Much higher radiation intensity
- Shorter peak wavelength

Examples:

- Furnace tubes
- Molten metal
- Flames

---

# Engineering Significance

Understanding the black body radiation curve helps engineers:

- Predict radiation heat transfer
- Understand infrared radiation
- Analyze furnace performance
- Design thermal imaging systems
- Select suitable radiation detectors

The curve forms the foundation for Wien's Law and Stefan-Boltzmann Law.

# Key Takeaways

- Radiation is emitted over a range of wavelengths.
- A black body does not emit radiation at a single wavelength.
- Radiation intensity varies with wavelength.
- The radiation distribution is represented by the black body radiation curve.
- Increasing temperature increases total radiation emission.
- Increasing temperature shifts the peak toward shorter wavelengths.
- The black body radiation curve forms the basis of radiation heat transfer analysis.

---

# Summary

A black body emits radiation over a continuous range of wavelengths rather than at a single wavelength. The distribution of emitted energy is represented by the black body radiation curve. As temperature increases, the total radiation emitted increases dramatically and the peak of the curve shifts toward shorter wavelengths. This behavior explains why hotter objects radiate more energy and forms the foundation for Wien's Displacement Law and Stefan-Boltzmann Law.
