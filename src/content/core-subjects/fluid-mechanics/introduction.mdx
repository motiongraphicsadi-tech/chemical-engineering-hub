# Introduction

Fluid Mechanics is one of the most important subjects in Chemical Engineering. It deals with the behavior of fluids (liquids and gases) at rest and in motion and provides the foundation for understanding fluid transport throughout industrial systems.

Every chemical plant continuously handles fluids through:

- Pipelines
- Pumps
- Reactors
- Heat Exchangers
- Distillation Columns
- Storage Tanks
- Utility Systems

Whether transporting crude oil through pipelines, circulating cooling water, feeding reactants into reactors, or moving products between processing units, fluid mechanics governs how these systems operate.

Understanding fluid mechanics allows engineers to predict fluid behavior, calculate flow rates, estimate pressure losses, select equipment, and design safe and efficient industrial processes.

---

# Why Fluid Mechanics Matters in Chemical Engineering

Chemical engineers rarely work with stationary materials.

Most industrial operations involve moving fluids.

Examples include:

### Process Fluid Transport

Transporting liquids and gases between process units.

### Reactor Feed Systems

Supplying reactants to chemical reactors.

### Heat Transfer Operations

Circulating fluids through heat exchangers.

### Separation Processes

Handling fluids in distillation, absorption, and extraction systems.

### Utility Networks

Managing steam, cooling water, compressed air, and condensate systems.

### Product Handling

Loading, unloading, and transferring finished products.

Without fluid mechanics, modern process industries would not function.

# Summary

Fluid Mechanics is the science of understanding how fluids behave at rest and in motion. It provides the foundation for designing pipelines, pumps, reactors, heat exchangers, and countless other industrial systems.

In this learning path, you will progress from basic fluid properties and pressure concepts to advanced topics such as Bernoulli's equation, pipe flow losses, Reynolds number, boundary layer theory, and industrial pump selection. By the end of the course, you will have the knowledge required to analyze, design, and troubleshoot fluid transport systems encountered throughout the chemical process industries.
