# Introduction

Fluids must often be transported from one location to another in industrial plants. This movement requires energy to overcome elevation differences, pressure requirements, and friction losses.

Pumps are mechanical devices used to add energy to fluids and move them through piping systems.

They are among the most important equipment used in:

- Chemical plants
- Petroleum refineries
- Water treatment facilities
- Power plants
- Food processing industries

Understanding pumps is essential for designing efficient fluid transport systems.

---

# Why It Matters

Almost every chemical process requires fluid movement.

Examples include:

- Feeding reactants to reactors
- Circulating cooling water
- Transferring products between units
- Supplying boiler feed water
- Moving slurries and viscous liquids

Proper pump selection helps:

- Reduce energy consumption
- Improve reliability
- Prevent cavitation
- Increase plant efficiency

Pump failures can result in production losses and safety concerns.

---

# Definition

A pump is a mechanical device that converts mechanical energy into fluid energy.

Its primary purpose is to:

- Increase fluid pressure
- Increase fluid velocity
- Move fluids through a system

Pumps add energy to the fluid and overcome system resistance.

---

# Physical Significance

According to Bernoulli's principle, fluid energy consists of:

- Pressure energy
- Kinetic energy
- Potential energy

A pump increases the total energy of the fluid.

This added energy allows fluid to:

- Flow through pipes
- Reach higher elevations
- Overcome pressure losses
- Supply process equipment

Without pumps, most industrial fluid transport systems would not function.

---

# Mathematical Definition

Pump power delivered to a fluid is:

$$
P_f = \rho g Q H
$$

Where:

- \(P_f\) = Fluid Power (W)
- \(\rho\) = Fluid Density
- \(g\) = Gravitational Acceleration
- \(Q\) = Volumetric Flow Rate
- \(H\) = Pump Head

This equation represents useful hydraulic power.

---

# Introduction to Pumps

Pumps are broadly classified into:

### Dynamic Pumps

Energy is continuously transferred to the fluid.

Example:

- Centrifugal Pumps

### Positive Displacement Pumps

A fixed volume of fluid is trapped and displaced.

Examples:

- Reciprocating Pumps
- Gear Pumps
- Screw Pumps

These two categories cover most industrial pumping applications.

---

# Centrifugal Pumps

A centrifugal pump is the most widely used industrial pump.

Working Principle:

1. Fluid enters the impeller eye.
2. Rotating impeller increases fluid velocity.
3. Velocity is converted into pressure in the casing.
4. Fluid exits at higher pressure.

Characteristics:

- Continuous flow
- Simple construction
- High flow rates
- Low maintenance

Applications:

- Water transport
- Chemical processing
- Cooling water systems
- Petroleum industries

---

# Positive Displacement Pumps

Positive displacement pumps deliver a fixed volume of fluid per cycle.

Working Principle:

1. Fluid enters a chamber.
2. Chamber volume decreases.
3. Fluid is forced out.

Types:

### Reciprocating Pumps

Use pistons or plungers.

### Gear Pumps

Use rotating gears.

### Screw Pumps

Use intermeshing screws.

Advantages:

- High pressure capability
- Accurate flow delivery
- Suitable for viscous fluids

---

# Pump Head

Pump head represents the energy added to the fluid.

It is usually expressed in meters of fluid.

Total pump head:

$$
H
=
\frac{P_2-P_1}{\rho g}
+
\frac{V_2^2-V_1^2}{2g}
+
(z_2-z_1)
$$

Where:

- \(P\) = Pressure
- \(V\) = Velocity
- \(z\) = Elevation

Pump head determines the capability of a pump.

---

# Pump Efficiency

Pump efficiency measures how effectively mechanical power is converted into hydraulic power.

$$
\eta
=
\frac{\text{Hydraulic Power}}
{\text{Input Power}}
\times 100
$$

Or:

$$
\eta
=
\frac{\rho gQH}{P_{input}}
\times 100
$$

Higher efficiency means lower energy consumption.

Typical centrifugal pump efficiencies:

$$
60\%-90\%
$$

---

# NPSH

NPSH stands for:

### Net Positive Suction Head

It indicates the pressure available at the pump suction above vapor pressure.

Two terms are used:

### NPSH Available (NPSHA)

Provided by the system.

### NPSH Required (NPSHR)

Specified by the pump manufacturer.

Safe operation requires:

$$
NPSHA > NPSHR
$$

---

# Cavitation

Cavitation occurs when local pressure falls below the fluid vapor pressure.

Consequences:

- Vapor bubble formation
- Bubble collapse
- Noise and vibration
- Reduced efficiency
- Equipment damage

Symptoms:

- Rattling sound
- Reduced flow rate
- Increased vibration

Prevention:

- Increase suction pressure
- Reduce fluid temperature
- Increase NPSH available

# Characteristic Curves

Pump performance is represented using characteristic curves.

Common curves include:

### Head vs Flow Rate

Shows head variation with discharge.

### Efficiency vs Flow Rate

Shows optimum operating point.

### Power vs Flow Rate

Shows energy requirements.

### NPSHR vs Flow Rate

Indicates cavitation limitations.

These curves help select the proper pump.

# Pump Selection

Pump selection depends on:

### Flow Rate Requirement

Required system discharge.

### Total Dynamic Head

Pressure and elevation requirements.

### Fluid Properties

- Density
- Viscosity
- Corrosiveness

### Operating Conditions

Temperature and pressure.

### Efficiency

Energy consumption considerations.

Proper pump selection improves reliability and operating economy.

# Industrial Pumping Systems

Industrial pumping systems consist of:

- Pump
- Motor
- Suction line
- Discharge line
- Valves
- Instrumentation

The pump must overcome:

- Static head
- Friction losses
- Process pressure requirements

System design directly affects pump performance.

# Chemical Plant Applications

Pumps are extensively used in:

### Reactor Feed Systems

Deliver reactants to reactors.

### Distillation Units

Transfer liquid products.

### Heat Exchangers

Circulate process fluids.

### Utility Systems

Move cooling water and steam condensate.

### Storage and Transfer Operations

Load and unload process liquids.

### Slurry Transport

Move solid-liquid mixtures.

# Factors Affecting It

Pump performance depends on:

### Flow Rate

Higher flow rates alter operating conditions.

### Fluid Viscosity

Viscous fluids reduce efficiency.

### Suction Conditions

Poor suction promotes cavitation.

### Pipe Losses

Increase required pump head.

### Impeller Speed

Directly affects pump performance.

# Common Mistakes

Students often:

- Confuse pressure and head
- Ignore cavitation risks
- Select pumps without system curve analysis
- Neglect NPSH requirements
- Assume higher flow always means better performance

<WarningBox>
A pump should operate near its Best Efficiency Point (BEP). Operating far from BEP reduces efficiency and may damage the pump.
</WarningBox>

# Summary

Pumps are essential devices used to transport fluids throughout industrial systems. They add energy to fluids and overcome elevation changes, pressure requirements, and friction losses.

Centrifugal pumps dominate most industrial applications, while positive displacement pumps are preferred for high-pressure and viscous fluid services. Understanding pump head, efficiency, NPSH, cavitation, and characteristic curves enables engineers to select and operate pumps safely and efficiently in chemical process industries.
