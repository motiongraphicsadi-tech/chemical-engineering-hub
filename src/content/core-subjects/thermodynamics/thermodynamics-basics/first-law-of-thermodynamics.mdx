# Introduction

The First Law of Thermodynamics is one of the most important principles in engineering science.

It is based on the law of conservation of energy, which states that energy cannot be created or destroyed.

Energy can only:

- Change form
- Transfer between systems
- Be stored

The First Law provides the foundation for all energy balance calculations used in chemical engineering.

---

# Why It Matters

Every industrial process involves energy transfer.

Examples include:

- Boilers generating steam
- Turbines producing power
- Compressors increasing pressure
- Heat exchangers transferring heat
- Pumps moving fluids

The First Law allows engineers to:

- Calculate energy requirements
- Determine equipment performance
- Design energy-efficient systems
- Analyze industrial processes

---

# Core Concept

The First Law is based on energy conservation.

Energy entering a system must equal:

- Energy leaving the system
- Plus any energy stored within the system

Mathematically:

$$
Energy\ In
-
Energy\ Out
=
Accumulation
$$

This principle applies to all thermodynamic systems.

---

# Working Principle

The First Law tracks energy interactions between:

- System
- Surroundings

Energy may cross the system boundary as:

- Heat
- Work
- Mass Flow

The energy balance is applied to determine system behavior during a process.

---

# Mathematical Definition

For a general system:

$$
\Delta E = Q - W
$$

Where:

- \(\Delta E\) = Change in Total Energy
- \(Q\) = Heat Added to System
- \(W\) = Work Done by System

This equation is the basic mathematical form of the First Law.

---

# Principle of Energy Conservation

The First Law is a statement of conservation of energy.

Energy cannot be:

- Created
- Destroyed

Energy can only be converted from one form to another.

Examples:

- Chemical Energy → Thermal Energy
- Thermal Energy → Mechanical Energy
- Mechanical Energy → Electrical Energy

Total energy remains conserved.

---

# Statement of First Law

The First Law states:

"Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form to another."

This law forms the basis of all energy balance calculations.

---

# Internal Energy Concept

Internal energy represents microscopic energy stored within a system.

Symbol:

$$
U
$$

It includes:

- Molecular kinetic energy
- Molecular potential energy
- Chemical energy

Change in internal energy is often written as:

$$
\Delta U
=
U_2-U_1
$$

---

# Heat

Heat is energy transferred due to temperature difference.

Symbol:

$$
Q
$$

Heat transfer occurs because of:

- Conduction
- Convection
- Radiation

Heat is not a property.

It is energy in transit.

---

# Work

Work is energy transferred when a force acts through a distance.

Symbol:

$$
W
$$

Examples:

- Turbine work
- Compressor work
- Shaft work
- Boundary work

Like heat, work is not a property.

It is energy crossing the system boundary.

---

# Sign Convention

The most common engineering sign convention is:

Heat added to system:

$$
Q > 0
$$

Heat removed from system:

$$
Q < 0
$$

Work done by system:

$$
W > 0
$$

Work done on system:

$$
W < 0
$$

Correct sign convention is essential in calculations.

---

# Energy Transfer Mechanisms

Energy may cross system boundaries through:

- Heat Transfer
- Work Transfer
- Mass Flow

These are the only mechanisms of energy transfer in thermodynamics.

---

# First Law for Closed Systems

A closed system does not allow mass transfer.

Energy balance:

$$
\Delta U
=
Q-W
$$

This equation is used extensively for:

- Piston-cylinder systems
- Closed containers
- Batch processes

---

# First Law for Cyclic Processes

For a cyclic process:

Initial State = Final State

Therefore:

$$
\Delta U = 0
$$

The First Law becomes:

$$
Q=W
$$

Net heat transfer equals net work transfer.

---

# First Law for Open Systems

Open systems allow mass flow across boundaries.

Examples:

- Turbines
- Compressors
- Pumps
- Nozzles

Additional energy terms must be considered:

- Enthalpy
- Kinetic Energy
- Potential Energy

---

# Steady Flow Energy Equation (SFEE)

For steady-flow systems:

$$
Q-W
=
\dot m
\left[
(h_2-h_1)
+
\frac{V_2^2-V_1^2}{2}
+
g(z_2-z_1)
\right]
$$

Where:

- \(h\) = Specific Enthalpy
- \(V\) = Velocity
- \(z\) = Elevation
- \(g\) = Gravitational Constant

SFEE is one of the most important equations in engineering thermodynamics.

---

# Industrial Applications

The First Law is used in:

- Boilers
- Turbines
- Compressors
- Pumps
- Nozzles
- Heat Exchangers
- Distillation Columns
- Refrigeration Systems

Virtually every energy-related industrial calculation involves the First Law.

---

# Turbines

Turbines convert fluid energy into mechanical work.

Typically:

$$
W_{out}>0
$$

Applications:

- Power plants
- Steam turbines
- Gas turbines

---

# Compressors

Compressors increase fluid pressure.

Typically:

$$
W_{in}>0
$$

Applications:

- Air compression
- Gas processing
- Refrigeration systems

---

# Pumps

Pumps increase liquid pressure.

Energy is supplied as shaft work.

Applications:

- Water systems
- Chemical plants
- Utility systems

---

# Nozzles

Nozzles convert pressure energy into velocity.

Generally:

$$
h_1 > h_2
$$

and

$$
V_2 > V_1
$$

Nozzles are analyzed using SFEE.

---

# Heat Exchangers

Heat exchangers transfer heat between fluids.

Typically:

$$
W=0
$$

Energy balance is based on enthalpy changes.

---

# Advantages

The First Law helps engineers:

- Perform energy balances
- Analyze equipment
- Design processes
- Predict energy requirements
- Improve efficiency

It provides the foundation for engineering thermodynamics.

---

# Limitations

The First Law cannot determine:

- Direction of heat flow
- Process feasibility
- Maximum efficiency
- Entropy generation

These topics are addressed by the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

---

# Engineering Perspective

Chemical engineers use the First Law daily.

Applications include:

- Energy balance calculations
- Process simulation
- Utility optimization
- Equipment design
- Process troubleshooting

A strong understanding of the First Law is essential for all advanced thermodynamics topics.

---

# Solved Example

A closed system receives:

$$
Q=500kJ
$$

and performs:

$$
W=200kJ
$$

Using:

$$
\Delta U = Q-W
$$

Substituting:

$$
\Delta U = 500-200
$$

$$
\Delta U = 300kJ
$$

Therefore:

$$
Energy\ Increase = 300kJ
$$

---

# Common Mistakes

Students commonly:

- Confuse heat and internal energy
- Use incorrect sign conventions
- Forget work terms
- Ignore kinetic energy changes
- Ignore potential energy changes

<WarningBox>
The First Law explains how much energy is transferred but does not explain whether a process can occur naturally.
</WarningBox>

---

# Key Takeaways

- The First Law is based on conservation of energy.
- Energy cannot be created or destroyed.
- Heat and work are energy transfer mechanisms.
- Internal energy is stored within a system.
- Closed systems use ΔU = Q − W.
- Cyclic processes have ΔU = 0.
- Open systems are analyzed using SFEE.
- The First Law is fundamental to engineering calculations.

# Summary

The First Law of Thermodynamics is the law of energy conservation.

It states that energy can neither be created nor destroyed and forms the basis of all energy balance calculations.

The law is applied to closed systems, open systems, turbines, compressors, pumps, nozzles, heat exchangers, and countless industrial processes, making it one of the most important concepts in engineering thermodynamics.
