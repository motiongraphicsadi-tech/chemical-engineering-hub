# Introduction

Industrial facilities occasionally experience equipment failures, process upsets, production losses, safety incidents, and near misses. While it is often easy to identify what happened, understanding why it happened is much more important.

Root Cause Analysis (RCA) is a systematic problem-solving method used to identify the underlying causes of an incident rather than focusing only on its immediate symptoms.

The goal of RCA is to eliminate the true cause of a problem so that similar incidents do not occur again.

<IndustrialInsight>
The most expensive mistake in troubleshooting is fixing the symptom while leaving the actual cause unresolved.
</IndustrialInsight>

---

# Why It Matters

Industrial incidents often result from multiple contributing factors.

For example:

- a pump fails
- a pipe leaks
- a reactor trips
- a compressor shuts down

Simply replacing damaged equipment may restore operation temporarily, but if the underlying cause remains, the same failure is likely to occur again.

RCA helps organizations:

- prevent recurring failures
- improve reliability
- reduce downtime
- improve safety
- strengthen operational performance

---

# Core Concept

The fundamental concept of RCA is:

> Every problem has one or more underlying causes that must be identified and corrected.

Instead of asking:

"What failed?"

RCA asks:

"Why did it fail?"

The investigation continues until the true system-level cause is identified.

This often reveals problems related to:

- design
- maintenance
- procedures
- training
- management systems

---

# Main Elements

A Root Cause Analysis generally includes several important elements.

## Problem Statement

A clear description of the incident being investigated.

Example:

"Centrifugal pump failed during normal operation."

---

## Evidence Collection

Information gathered from:

- inspections
- maintenance records
- operating logs
- interviews
- process data

---

## Immediate Cause

The direct cause of the incident.

Example:

Bearing failure.

---

## Contributing Causes

Factors that supported the failure.

Examples:

- poor lubrication
- vibration
- misalignment

---

## Root Cause

The underlying reason the incident occurred.

Examples:

- inadequate maintenance program
- missing inspection procedures
- insufficient training

---

## Corrective Actions

Actions implemented to prevent recurrence.

Examples:

- maintenance improvements
- procedure revisions
- operator training

---

# Common RCA Techniques

Several techniques are commonly used during investigations.

## 5 Whys Method

The investigator repeatedly asks:

"Why?"

until the root cause is identified.

Example:

Pump Failed  
↓  
Why?  
Bearing Damaged  
↓  
Why?  
Poor Lubrication  
↓  
Why?  
Lubrication Schedule Missed  
↓  
Why?  
Maintenance Procedure Not Followed  
↓  
Why?  
Inadequate Maintenance Management

Root Cause Identified.

---

## Fishbone Diagram

Also called the:

# Ishikawa Diagram

It organizes possible causes into categories such as:

- People
- Equipment
- Methods
- Materials
- Environment
- Management

---

## Fault Tree Analysis

A logical diagram used to identify combinations of failures that lead to an event.

# Industrial Example

Consider a centrifugal pump failure.

## Problem

Pump seized during operation.

---

## Immediate Cause

Bearing failure.

---

## Contributing Causes

- overheating
- excessive vibration
- poor lubrication

---

## Investigation Findings

Maintenance records show lubrication intervals were frequently missed.

---

## Root Cause

Preventive maintenance scheduling system was ineffective.

---

## Corrective Actions

- improve maintenance planning
- implement inspection tracking
- train maintenance personnel

---

## Result

Future bearing failures become less likely.

# Difference Between Symptoms and Root Causes

One of the most important concepts in RCA is distinguishing symptoms from causes.

Example:

| Observation | Type |
|-------------|------|
| Pump stopped | Symptom |
| Bearing failed | Immediate Cause |
| Poor lubrication | Contributing Cause |
| Weak maintenance program | Root Cause |

Focusing only on symptoms rarely prevents recurrence.

# Summary

Root Cause Analysis (RCA) is a systematic investigation technique used to identify the underlying causes of failures, incidents, and process problems. Instead of focusing only on immediate symptoms, RCA seeks to understand why an event occurred and what system weaknesses allowed it to happen.

By using methods such as the 5 Whys, Fishbone Diagrams, and Fault Tree Analysis, engineers can develop effective corrective actions that prevent recurrence. RCA is an essential skill for chemical engineers because it improves safety, reliability, operational performance, and long-term problem-solving capability.
