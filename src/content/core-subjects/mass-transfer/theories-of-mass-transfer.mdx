# Theories of Mass Transfer Introduction

Mass transfer frequently occurs between two phases such as:

- Gas and liquid
- Liquid and solid
- Gas and solid

Although diffusion is the fundamental transport mechanism, engineers require simplified models to predict actual mass transfer rates in industrial equipment.

To explain how molecules move across phase boundaries, several mass transfer theories have been developed.

The most important theories are:

- Film Theory
- Penetration Theory
- Surface Renewal Theory

These theories provide the foundation for understanding mass transfer coefficients used in industrial design.

---

Mass transfer theories help engineers:

- Predict transfer rates

# Core Concept

Mass transfer occurs because of concentration differences.

However, real systems involve:

- Fluid motion
- Turbulence
- Interfacial resistance
- Boundary layers

Mass transfer theories simplify these complex phenomena into mathematical models.

Their objective is to estimate:

$$
N_A
=
k(C_1-C_2)
$$

Where:

- \(N_A\) = Mass Transfer Flux
- \(k\) = Mass Transfer Coefficient
- \(C_1-C_2\) = Driving Force

Each theory differs in how it models the fluid near the interface.

---

# Classification and Types

The major mass transfer theories are:

## Film Theory

Developed by Whitman.

Assumes a stagnant film near the interface.

---

## Penetration Theory

Developed by Higbie.

Assumes fluid elements remain at the interface for a short time.

---

## Surface Renewal Theory

Developed by Danckwerts.

Assumes continuous replacement of fluid elements at the interface.

---

# Working Principle

All theories attempt to explain:

1. How molecules reach the interface.
2. How diffusion occurs near the interface.
3. How concentration gradients develop.
4. How mass transfer coefficients are generated.

The difference lies in their assumptions regarding fluid motion near the interface.

---

# Mathematical Definition

General mass transfer equation:

$$
N_A
=
k(C_b-C_i)
$$

Where:

| Symbol | Description | Unit |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| $N_A$ | Mass Transfer Flux | mol/m²·s |
| $k$ | Mass Transfer Coefficient | m/s |
| $C_b$ | Bulk Concentration | mol/m³ |
| $C_i$ | Interface Concentration | mol/m³ |

Mass transfer theories provide expressions for \(k\).

---

# Assumptions

Although assumptions differ among theories, common assumptions include:

- Isothermal conditions
- Constant diffusivity
- No chemical reaction
- Uniform interface
- One-dimensional diffusion

Additional assumptions depend on the specific theory.

---

# Derivation and Theory

# Film Theory

## Concept

Film Theory assumes that a thin stagnant film exists adjacent to the interface.

Inside the film:

- Fluid motion is negligible.
- Mass transfer occurs only by molecular diffusion.

Outside the film:

- Fluid is perfectly mixed.
- Concentration remains uniform.

---

## Assumptions

- Thin stagnant film
- Steady-state diffusion
- Constant film thickness
- Constant diffusivity

---

## Mathematical Model

Using Fick's Law:

$$
N_A
=
D_{AB}
\frac{C_b-C_i}
{\delta}
$$

Where:

- \(\delta\) = Film Thickness

Mass transfer coefficient:

$$
k
=
\frac{D_{AB}}
{\delta}
$$

---

## Physical Significance

A thinner film gives:

$$
k \uparrow
$$

and higher mass transfer rates.

---

# Penetration Theory

## Concept

Proposed by Higbie.

Fluid elements are continuously brought to the interface and remain there for a finite contact time.

During this exposure:

- Unsteady diffusion occurs.
- Molecules penetrate into the fluid.

---

## Assumptions

- Unsteady-state diffusion
- Equal exposure time
- Fresh fluid reaches the interface
- No concentration gradient initially

---

## Mathematical Model

Higbie's equation:

$$
k
=
2
\sqrt{
\frac{D_{AB}}
{\pi t}
}
$$

Where:

- \(t\) = Contact Time

---

## Physical Significance

Shorter contact time:

- Steeper concentration gradients
- Higher transfer rates

---

# Surface Renewal Theory

## Concept

Proposed by Danckwerts.

Fluid elements are continuously replaced at random intervals.

Unlike penetration theory:

- Exposure times are not equal.
- Surface elements renew continuously.

This theory better represents turbulent systems.

---

## Assumptions

- Random surface renewal
- Unsteady diffusion
- Continuous replacement of fluid elements

---

## Mathematical Model

Mass transfer coefficient:

$$
k
=
\sqrt{D_{AB}s}
$$

Where:

- \(s\) = Surface Renewal Rate

---

## Physical Significance

Higher renewal rates produce larger mass transfer coefficients.

# Graphical Interpretation

## Film Theory

Concentration profile:

- Linear
- Steady-state

Concentration decreases uniformly across the film.

---

## Penetration Theory

Concentration profile:

- Curved
- Time dependent

Diffusion depth increases with time.

---

## Surface Renewal Theory

Concentration profiles continuously change because surface elements are replaced.

This better represents turbulent industrial systems.

## Penetration Theory

- Assumes equal exposure times

## Surface Renewal Theory

# Solved Example

### Problem

For a gas absorption system:

$$
D_{AB}
=
2\times10^{-5}
\;m^2/s
$$

Film thickness:

$$
\delta
=
0.002
\;m
$$

Calculate the mass transfer coefficient using Film Theory.

### Given

- \(D_{AB}=2\times10^{-5}\;m^2/s\)
- \(\delta=0.002\;m\)

### Solution

Using Film Theory:

$$
k
=
\frac{D_{AB}}
{\delta}
$$

Substituting:

$$
k
=
\frac{2\times10^{-5}}
{0.002}
$$

$$
k
=
1\times10^{-2}
\;m/s
$$

### Final Answer

$$
k
=
0.01
\;m/s
$$

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing film thickness with diffusion distance
- Using steady-state equations for transient diffusion
- Ignoring turbulence effects
- Mixing penetration and surface renewal assumptions
- Forgetting units of mass transfer coefficient

---

# Key Takeaways

- Mass transfer theories explain interphase mass transfer.
- Film Theory assumes a stagnant diffusion film.
- Penetration Theory assumes finite exposure time.
- Surface Renewal Theory assumes continuous surface replacement.
- All theories provide mass transfer coefficients.
- Film Theory is the simplest and most widely used.
- Surface Renewal Theory best represents turbulent systems.
- These theories form the basis of industrial mass transfer design.

# Summary

Mass transfer theories provide simplified models for understanding how species move across phase boundaries. Film Theory assumes diffusion through a stagnant film, Penetration Theory models finite exposure of fluid elements to the interface, and Surface Renewal Theory considers continuous replacement of surface elements.

These theories form the theoretical basis for mass transfer coefficients used in the design of absorbers, distillation columns, extractors, dryers, and numerous industrial mass transfer operations.
