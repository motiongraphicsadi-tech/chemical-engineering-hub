# Infrared Radiation

<TopicRoadmap />


## Why can an infrared camera detect hot equipment?

When maintenance engineers point an infrared camera at a steam pipe, motor, bearing, or furnace, the camera can "see" temperature differences that are invisible to the human eye.

How?

Because hot objects emit **infrared radiation**.

Infrared cameras detect this radiation and convert it into thermal images.

---

# What Is Infrared Radiation?

Infrared (IR) radiation is a region of the electromagnetic spectrum located just beyond visible red light.

It is the primary form of thermal radiation emitted by most engineering equipment.

Infrared radiation:

- Cannot be seen by the human eye.
- Carries thermal energy.
- Is emitted by all objects above 0 K.

---

# Infrared Radiation from Common Objects

Different objects emit radiation at different wavelengths depending on their temperature.

| Object | Peak Wavelength |
|----------|----------|
| Human Body | ~9 μm |
| Steam Pipe | ~5 μm |
| Furnace Surface | ~2–3 μm |

Notice:

- Cooler objects emit longer wavelengths.
- Hotter objects emit shorter wavelengths.

# Why Infrared Radiation Matters

Most industrial equipment operates in temperature ranges where the majority of emitted radiation falls within the infrared region.

Examples:

- Steam pipelines
- Heat exchangers
- Reactors
- Furnaces
- Boilers
- Motors
- Electrical panels

As a result, infrared radiation becomes an extremely useful diagnostic tool.

---

# Industrial Application: Predictive Maintenance

Infrared cameras allow engineers to identify problems before equipment fails.

Examples:

- Overheating motors
- Hot bearings
- Electrical faults
- Blocked heat exchanger tubes

Early detection reduces:

- Downtime
- Maintenance costs
- Safety risks

---

# Industrial Application: Hotspot Detection

Abnormally hot regions often indicate developing problems.

Infrared imaging helps detect:

- Electrical hotspots
- Overloaded circuits
- Friction heating
- Mechanical failures

Hotspots can be identified long before visible damage occurs.

---

# Industrial Application: Insulation Inspection

Damaged insulation causes excessive heat loss.

Infrared cameras can quickly identify:

- Missing insulation
- Wet insulation
- Insulation failures
- Thermal bridges

This helps improve:

- Energy efficiency
- Process reliability
- Operating costs

---

# Engineering Perspective

When a process engineer observes thermal images:

- Bright regions indicate higher temperatures.
- Dark regions indicate lower temperatures.
- Temperature variations reveal energy losses and equipment problems.

Infrared radiation provides a non-contact method for monitoring plant health.

# Key Takeaways

- Infrared radiation is the primary form of thermal radiation emitted by industrial equipment.
- Infrared radiation is invisible to the human eye.
- Human bodies emit radiation around 9 μm.
- Steam pipes typically emit around 5 μm.
- Furnaces often emit around 2–3 μm.
- Infrared cameras detect thermal radiation and convert it into thermal images.
- Infrared imaging is widely used for predictive maintenance, hotspot detection, and insulation inspection.

# Summary

Infrared radiation is the dominant form of thermal radiation emitted by most engineering equipment. As temperature increases, the emitted wavelength becomes shorter. Human bodies emit radiation around 9 μm, steam pipes around 5 μm, and furnaces around 2–3 μm. Infrared cameras detect this radiation and are widely used in industry for predictive maintenance, hotspot detection, and insulation inspection.
