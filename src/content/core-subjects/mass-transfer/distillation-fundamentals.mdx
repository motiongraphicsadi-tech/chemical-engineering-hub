# Distillation Fundamentals Introduction

Distillation is the most important separation process used in chemical, petrochemical, pharmaceutical, food, and petroleum industries.

The process separates components based on differences in volatility by repeatedly contacting vapor and liquid phases.

Distillation is responsible for:

- Crude oil fractionation
- Solvent recovery
- Alcohol purification
- Chemical production
- Pharmaceutical manufacturing

It is estimated that distillation accounts for a significant portion of the energy consumed in the chemical process industries.

Understanding distillation fundamentals is essential for every chemical engineer.

---

# Why It Matters

Distillation is important because it enables:

- Product purification
- Component recovery
- Feedstock preparation
- Waste minimization
- Process optimization

Industrial applications include:

- Petroleum refining
- Petrochemicals
- Fertilizer plants
- Food processing
- Pharmaceutical production

Distillation remains one of the most widely used mass transfer operations worldwide.

---

# Core Concept

Distillation works because different components have different volatilities.

The more volatile component:

- Vaporizes more easily
- Appears in higher concentration in vapor phase

The less volatile component:

- Remains primarily in liquid phase

Separation is achieved through repeated vaporization and condensation.

The driving force for distillation originates from differences in vapor-liquid equilibrium behavior.

---

# Classification and Types

## Flash Distillation

Single-stage separation.

Part of the liquid feed is vaporized instantly.

---

## Simple Distillation

Single vaporization-condensation step.

Suitable when volatility difference is large.

---

## Fractional Distillation

Multiple equilibrium stages.

Provides high-purity separations.

Most common industrial method.

---

## Steam Distillation

Uses steam to lower boiling temperature.

Used for heat-sensitive materials.

Examples:

- Essential oils
- Fragrances
- Natural products

---

# Working Principle

General distillation process:

1. Feed enters the system.
2. Heat is supplied.
3. Vaporization occurs.
4. Vapor becomes enriched in volatile components.
5. Condensation occurs.
6. Products are collected separately.

Repeated vapor-liquid contact improves separation.

---

# Mathematical Definition

Distillation calculations are based on vapor-liquid equilibrium.

General equilibrium relation:

$$
y_i
=
K_i x_i
$$

Where:

- \(y_i\) = Vapor mole fraction
- \(x_i\) = Liquid mole fraction
- \(K_i\) = Equilibrium constant

---

# Assumptions

Common assumptions include:

- Equilibrium stages
- Constant pressure
- Negligible heat losses
- Ideal mixing
- Steady-state operation
- Constant molar overflow (when applicable)

These assumptions simplify analysis and design.

# Derivation and Theory

# Vapor-Liquid Equilibrium (VLE)

Distillation depends on VLE.

At equilibrium:

$$
y_i
=
K_i x_i
$$

The vapor composition differs from liquid composition.

This difference creates separation.

---

# Relative Volatility

Relative volatility measures separation ease.

For components A and B:

$$
\alpha_{AB}
=
\frac{K_A}{K_B}
$$

Where:

- \(\alpha_{AB}\) = Relative volatility

Interpretation:

$$
\alpha > 1
$$

Component A is more volatile.

---

### Significance

Higher relative volatility:

- Easier separation
- Fewer stages required

Lower relative volatility:

- More difficult separation
- More stages required

---

# Flash Distillation Theory

A liquid feed is partially vaporized.

At equilibrium:

$$
F = V + L
$$

Where:

- \(F\) = Feed
- \(V\) = Vapor
- \(L\) = Liquid

The vapor becomes richer in the volatile component.

---

# Simple Distillation Theory

Single equilibrium stage.

Suitable for:

- Small-scale separations
- Large boiling-point differences

---

# Fractional Distillation Theory

Multiple equilibrium stages improve separation.

Each stage performs:

- Vaporization
- Condensation

Repeated enrichment produces high-purity products.

---

# Steam Distillation Theory

Total pressure:

$$
P
=
P_A
+
P_{steam}
$$

Boiling occurs when:

$$
P
=
P_{atm}
$$

This allows separation at temperatures below the normal boiling point.

# Solved Example

### Problem

For a binary mixture:

$$
K_A=2.4
$$

$$
K_B=1.2
$$

Calculate relative volatility.

### Given

- $K_A = 2.4$
- $K_B = 1.2$

### Solution

Using:

$$
\alpha
=
\frac{K_A}{K_B}
$$

Substituting:

$$
\alpha
=
\frac{2.4}{1.2}
$$

$$
\alpha
=
2.0
$$

### Final Answer

$$
\alpha=2.0
$$

The mixture is reasonably easy to separate.

# Summary

Distillation is the most widely used separation process in chemical engineering. It separates components based on differences in volatility through repeated vapor-liquid equilibrium stages. Key concepts include relative volatility, vapor-liquid equilibrium, and stage-wise separation.

Flash distillation, simple distillation, fractional distillation, and steam distillation serve different industrial purposes. Understanding these fundamentals is essential for designing efficient separation systems and optimizing industrial operations.
