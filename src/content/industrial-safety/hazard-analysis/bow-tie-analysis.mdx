# Introduction

Bow-Tie Analysis is a visual risk assessment technique widely used in process industries to understand how industrial accidents occur and how they can be prevented.

The method combines ideas from:

- Fault Tree Analysis (FTA)
- Event Tree Analysis (ETA)

into a single easy-to-understand diagram.

The shape resembles a bow tie, which is why it is called **Bow-Tie Analysis**.

<IndustrialInsight>
Major industries such as oil & gas, petrochemical, chemical manufacturing, and offshore facilities use Bow-Tie Analysis to manage high-risk operations and prevent catastrophic incidents.
</IndustrialInsight>

---

# Why Bow-Tie Analysis is Important

Industrial accidents rarely occur because of a single failure.

Most accidents happen when:

- hazards are present
- threats are not controlled
- safety barriers fail
- consequences escalate

Bow-Tie Analysis helps engineers visualize these relationships and understand where protection systems are required.

# Hazard

A hazard is anything with the potential to cause harm.

Examples include:

- flammable hydrocarbons
- toxic chemicals
- high-pressure systems
- high-temperature fluids
- stored energy

The hazard exists continuously within the process.

---

# Top Event

The top event is the moment when control over the hazard is lost.

Examples:

- loss of containment
- toxic gas release
- vessel overpressure
- pump seal failure
- pipeline rupture

The entire Bow-Tie diagram is built around the top event.

<WarningBox>
The top event is not the accident itself. It is the loss of control that can lead to multiple accident scenarios.
</WarningBox>

---

# Threats

Threats are events or conditions that can cause the top event to occur.

Examples:

- corrosion
- operator error
- equipment failure
- overpressure
- poor maintenance
- process upset

Threats appear on the left side of the Bow-Tie.

---

# Preventive Barriers

Preventive barriers stop threats from causing the top event.

Examples:

- inspections
- alarms
- maintenance programs
- pressure control systems
- operating procedures
- training programs

These barriers reduce the likelihood of losing control over the hazard.

---

# Consequences

Consequences are outcomes that may occur after the top event happens.

Examples:

- fire
- explosion
- toxic exposure
- environmental damage
- production loss
- equipment damage

Consequences appear on the right side of the Bow-Tie.

---

# Mitigation Barriers

Mitigation barriers reduce the severity of consequences after the top event occurs.

Examples:

- firewater systems
- emergency shutdown systems
- gas detectors
- relief valves
- emergency response plans
- evacuation procedures

These barriers do not prevent the event but reduce its impact.

---

# Example: Pump Seal Failure

Consider a centrifugal pump handling a flammable hydrocarbon.

## Hazard

Flammable hydrocarbon inventory.

## Threats

- seal wear
- vibration
- poor maintenance
- misalignment

## Preventive Barriers

- condition monitoring
- vibration monitoring
- maintenance program
- seal inspection

## Top Event

Hydrocarbon leakage from pump seal.

## Mitigation Barriers

- gas detectors
- firewater system
- emergency shutdown
- firefighting team

## Consequences

- fire
- explosion
- injury
- production shutdown

This creates a complete Bow-Tie representation of the risk.

# Bow-Tie Analysis Process

Industries generally follow these steps:

## Step 1

Identify the hazard.

## Step 2

Define the top event.

## Step 3

Identify threats.

## Step 4

Identify preventive barriers.

## Step 5

Identify consequences.

## Step 6

Identify mitigation barriers.

## Step 7

Review barrier effectiveness.

# Limitations of Bow-Tie Analysis

Like all tools, Bow-Tie Analysis has limitations.

<div className="bg-yellow-950/30 border border-yellow-700 rounded-xl p-5 my-6">

- Qualitative in nature
- Depends on expert knowledge
- Can become complex for large systems
- Requires periodic updates

# Bow-Tie Analysis in Process Safety

Bow-Tie Analysis is commonly used for:

- process safety management
- HAZOP follow-up
- major accident prevention
- safety case development
- barrier management

Industries use it to verify that critical safety barriers remain effective throughout plant operation.

---

# Relationship with Other Safety Tools

Bow-Tie Analysis often works together with:

- HAZOP
- Fault Tree Analysis
- Event Tree Analysis
- LOPA
- Risk Matrix

Each tool serves a different role within the process safety lifecycle.

---

# Engineering Perspective

Chemical engineers use Bow-Tie Analysis to:

- understand accident scenarios
- identify critical barriers
- improve plant safety
- support investigations
- strengthen risk management programs

The method helps bridge the gap between engineering systems and safety management.

# Key Takeaways

- Bow-Tie Analysis is a visual risk management tool.
- It combines concepts from Fault Trees and Event Trees.
- Hazards, threats, top events, barriers, and consequences are the main elements.
- Preventive barriers stop incidents from occurring.
- Mitigation barriers reduce accident consequences.
- Bow-Tie Analysis is widely used in process safety management.
