# Gas Absorption and Stripping Introduction

Gas absorption and stripping are among the most important gas-liquid mass transfer operations used in chemical industries.

In these operations, a component is transferred between a gas phase and a liquid phase through direct contact.

Examples include:

- Removal of CO₂ from natural gas
- Ammonia recovery
- SO₂ removal from flue gases
- Oxygen removal from water
- Solvent recovery systems

These processes are performed in specialized equipment such as packed towers and tray towers.

Gas absorption and stripping form the backbone of many environmental, petrochemical, and chemical processing operations.

---

Gas absorption and stripping are essential because they enable:

- Gas purification

Industrial applications include:

- Refineries

These operations are among the most widely used separation processes in chemical engineering.

# Core Concept

Gas absorption and stripping are opposite processes.

## Gas Absorption

A solute transfers from a gas phase into a liquid phase.

Example:

$$
CO_2(g)
\rightarrow
CO_2(l)
$$

---

## Stripping

A solute transfers from a liquid phase into a gas phase.

Example:

$$
NH_3(l)
\rightarrow
NH_3(g)
$$

Both operations rely on:

- Mass transfer
- Gas-liquid equilibrium
- Concentration driving force

---

# Classification and Types

## Physical Absorption

Solute dissolves physically in the liquid.

Examples:

- CO₂ absorption in water
- Ammonia absorption in water

---

## Chemical Absorption

Chemical reaction occurs during absorption.

Examples:

- CO₂ absorption using MEA
- H₂S removal using amine solutions

---

## Air Stripping

Uses air to remove volatile components.

Examples:

- VOC removal
- Ammonia stripping

---

## Steam Stripping

Uses steam as stripping medium.

Examples:

- Solvent recovery
- Hydrocarbon removal

---

# Working Principle

## Absorption Process

1. Gas enters the column.
2. Liquid solvent enters from the opposite end.
3. Gas and liquid contact each other.
4. Solute diffuses from gas to liquid.
5. Purified gas exits.
6. Rich solvent leaves the column.

---

## Stripping Process

1. Liquid feed enters the column.
2. Gas or steam enters countercurrently.
3. Solute transfers from liquid to gas.
4. Purified liquid exits.
5. Solute-rich gas leaves the column.

# Mathematical Definition

Mass transfer flux:

$$
N_A
=
K_G(P_A-P_A^*)
$$

Where:

- \(N_A\) = Flux
- \(K_G\) = Overall gas-side coefficient
- \(P_A\) = Bulk gas partial pressure
- \(P_A^*\) = Equilibrium pressure

---

Liquid-side form:

$$
N_A
=
K_L(C_A^*-C_A)
$$

Where:

- \(C_A^*\) = Equilibrium concentration
- \(C_A\) = Actual concentration

---

# Assumptions

Common assumptions include:

- Steady-state operation
- Constant temperature
- Constant pressure
- Equilibrium at interface
- No accumulation
- Uniform flow distribution

These assumptions simplify column analysis.

---

# Derivation and Theory

# Gas Absorption Theory

Mass transfer occurs because:

$$
P_A>P_A^*
$$

The solute concentration in gas exceeds equilibrium concentration.

Result:

Gas → Liquid transfer.

---

# Stripping Theory

Mass transfer occurs because:

$$
C_A>C_A^*
$$

The solute concentration in liquid exceeds equilibrium concentration.

Result:

Liquid → Gas transfer.

---

# Countercurrent Contact

Most industrial columns operate countercurrently.

Advantages:

- Higher driving force
- Better efficiency
- Lower solvent consumption

Countercurrent flow maximizes separation performance.

---

# Packed Tower Operation

Packed towers contain packing materials such as:

- Raschig rings
- Pall rings
- Berl saddles
- Structured packing

Functions:

- Increase interfacial area
- Improve mass transfer
- Reduce pressure drop

Packed towers are widely used for absorption and stripping.

---

# Tray Tower Operation

Tray columns contain multiple trays where gas and liquid contact occurs.

Common tray types:

- Bubble cap trays
- Sieve trays
- Valve trays

Advantages:

- Better stage-wise control
- Suitable for large capacities

# Key Relationships and Equations

## Overall Gas-Side Equation

$$
N_A
=
K_G(P_A-P_A^*)
$$

---

## Overall Liquid-Side Equation

$$
N_A
=
K_L(C_A^*-C_A)
$$

---

## Operating Line

For absorption:

$$
y
=
\frac{L}{G}x + C
$$

Where:

- \(L\) = Liquid flow rate
- \(G\) = Gas flow rate

---

## Equilibrium Relation

$$
y^*=mx
$$

Where:

- \(m\) = Equilibrium slope

---

## Driving Force

Gas basis:

$$
(y-y^*)
$$

Liquid basis:

$$
(x^*-x)
$$

# Solved Example

### Problem

A gas absorption system has:

$$
K_G
=
0.05
\;mol/(m^2.s.atm)
$$

Bulk gas partial pressure:

$$
P_A
=
0.40
\;atm
$$

Equilibrium pressure:

$$
P_A^*
=
0.10
\;atm
$$

Calculate mass transfer flux.

### Given

- \(K_G=0.05\)
- \(P_A=0.40\;atm\)
- \(P_A^*=0.10\;atm\)

### Solution

Using:

$$
N_A
=
K_G(P_A-P_A^*)
$$

Substituting:

$$
N_A
=
0.05(0.40-0.10)
$$

$$
N_A
=
0.015
\;mol/m^2.s
$$

### Final Answer

$$
N_A
=
0.015
\;mol/m^2.s
$$
